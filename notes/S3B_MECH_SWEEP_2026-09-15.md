### 2a. Candidate 1 — Load-priced KV eviction → `OCCUPIED`

Candidate 1 was `PARTIALLY OCCUPIED` in revision 1 on the strength of Marconi. It is now **`OCCUPIED`**. The decisive artifact is **KVLearn**, *To Keep or Not to Keep: Learning KV Cache Retention in Disaggregated LLM Serving Systems* (Liu, Yu, Jiang, Wang, Wu), **SYSTOR '26**, ACM, pp. 52–65, DOI `10.1145/3793230.3837769`. Its score is the candidate's inequality, term for term:

| Candidate 1's stated mechanism | KVLearn's shipped term |
|---|---|
| `P(reuse) ×` | `P̂(b)` from a **Prefix Reuse Predictor** learned online from delayed reuse labels |
| `(live marginal cost of recomputing it)` | `R(b) ≈ α_bw·L` if `L < L×` (bandwidth-bound), `≈ α_flop·L²` if `L ≥ L×` (compute-bound) — a **regime-aware recompute price with an explicit bandwidth→compute crossover** |
| `− residency cost` | `− U(b,Δt) = γ·|b|·Δt` with `γ = R̄·λ / M_S` — i.e. **live arrival rate λ and pool pressure M_S** |
| `< ...` threshold | `KEEP ⇔ CARS > θ`, θ from an **Adaptive Threshold Controller driven by closed-loop pool pressure and hit rate** |

The candidate contrasts its live price against "a static estimate". KVLearn's abstract asserts the opposite of static: *"This architectural shift invalidates a core assumption of classical cache policies: that the cost of a miss is simply recomputation on the same device."* And its recompute term is split at a calibrated regime boundary — structurally the same idea as the target box's batch ≈ 28 crossover, with the README recording *"A100 + LLaMA-3-8B calib: α_bw≈0.026 ms/tok, α_flop≈8e−6 ms/tok², L×=512"*.

**The one surviving condition.** In KVLearn, `R(b)` — the *price* — is a calibrated function of prefix length `L`, **not** of the instantaneous batch size or prefill/decode mix at the decision instant. The live load enters through the *residency* term `U` (via λ and M_S) and through the *threshold* θ (ATC), not through the price. Strict residue: *"price the recompute term by the instantaneous (batch, phase-mix) state, rather than by a calibrated length-keyed regime function."*

**This residue is documented, not recommended**, for three reasons: (i) the candidate's own framing contrasts live pricing against "a static estimate", and KVLearn's price is *not* static, so the framing as written is refuted; (ii) the live signals KVLearn does use (λ, M_S) are arguably more predictive for a *retention* decision than instantaneous batch composition; (iii) independent evidence (arXiv 2608.23658) suggests the instantaneous prefill price is nearly flat in the batch/phase mix on real hardware — median TTFT differs by ~1% between chunk sizes of 8192 and 32768 — so keying the price on that signal may be behaviourally vacuous. **If the team wants to keep a sliver, the only defensible experiment is a victim-ranking A/B: live-(batch, phase-mix)-priced vs KVLearn's length-keyed `R(b)` on the target box, showing the rankings differ and the difference costs measurable TTFT.** I did not find such an A/B.

**Corroborating occupants (all verified by me).** Marconi (arXiv 2411.19379) — strongest pre-SYSTOR occupant: *"Marconi introduces a new FLOP-aware eviction policy that assesses candidates for eviction based not only on recency/popularity, but also the potential compute savings they deliver (normalized against the space they consume in the cache)."* Its `flop_efficiency` is static and its `α` is fitted offline `[self-via-sub]`. **GreedyDual-Size** (Cao & Irani, USITS'97) — the classical spine: `H = cost / size`, `c(p)` a static per-object fetch cost `[self]`. **Online Weighted Paging with Unknown Weights** (arXiv 2410.21266, NeurIPS 2024; venue read back by me at `neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94374`) — the closest classical neighbour on the unknown-price axis: *"we present the first algorithm for online weighted paging that does not know page weights in advance, but rather learns from weight samples."* It learns a **per-page scalar**, not a system-state shadow price `[self]`. **PEEK** (2607.02525) — the live-state half without the price `[self-via-sub]`. **CacheWise** (2606.16824) — the `P(reuse)` half `[self]`. **Keepalive Economics** (2607.19214) — a priced break-even but with an *assumed-constant* price from a list-price table, and client-side rather than an eviction policy `[self]`. **vLLM RFC #23641** — proposed exactly `freq × compute_cost`, **closed as not planned, never implemented**, and it explicitly discards the load/time factor `[self-via-sub]`. **Production reality in the target engine:** preemption victim by lowest priority / last-in-running (`preempted_req = self.running[-1]`); prefix-cache blocks plain LRU `[self-via-sub]`.

**What would kill the residue:** (1) any eviction score — LLM or classical — in which the recompute/retrieval **price term itself** is a function of the instantaneous server load or queue state (a "load-priced GreedyDual"); (2) a victim-ranking experiment showing live-(batch, phase-mix) pricing and KVLearn's length-keyed `R(b)` give the **same eviction order** on the target box; (3) the full KVLearn paper text showing `R(b)` is already parameterised by the live batch mix (I could not read the full text — §3).

### 2b. Candidate 2 — Crossover-aware optimization selection → `PARTIALLY OCCUPIED` (narrowest of the seven)

> **Nearest neighbour X does A; under condition C it misses B; we do B, therefore when C holds the conclusion differs.**
>
> Nearest neighbour **PMPD (arXiv 2410.13461) does A = select an optimization along the compute/memory asymmetry of the two phases**, explicitly reasoning about *"the distinct redundancy, sensitivity to approximations, and arithmetic intensity of the prefill and decoding phases"* and stating the asymmetry directly (*"the prefill phase is known to be compute-bound, while the decoding stage is memory-bound"*); **under condition C = the binding constraint is set jointly by (batch, context) rather than by phase alone**, which the target box exhibits because its non-bandwidth term grows ~linearly with batch (2.79 → 12.72 ms, slope ≈ 0.158 ms/seq/step) and puts the bandwidth→compute crossover at batch ≈ 28 while KV bytes scale as n·L, **it misses B = a selection rule driven by position relative to a measured crossover** — PMPD's decision axes are **phase and decode depth**, never batch or context, and it never leaves the byte-reduction family; **we do B** (read the live (batch, context) position against the measured crossover and choose between byte-reduction and FLOP-reduction *families* for the same model and workload), **therefore when C holds the chosen family differs.**

**How thin this is — the adversarial reading.** The neighbourhood is dense on both halves, and the candidate survives only on their *conjunction*:

- **Bottleneck-informed technique selection: done.** PMPD `[self-via-sub]`; and I verified myself that `crossover` occurs **0 times** in PMPD's saved HTML while `arithmetic intensity` occurs once — so the selection is asymmetry-informed but not crossover-informed. (A subagent reported `roofline`=0 for this file; the true count is **1**, and it is inside a *bibliography entry* — the reference *LLM Inference Unveiled: Survey and Roofline Model Insights*. The substantive claim holds, but the corrected count is recorded here.)
- **Deriving a rule from the asymmetry: done.** *Hardware Co-Design Scaling Laws via Roofline Modelling for On-Device LLMs* (arXiv 2602.10377, 2026/02/10, title read back ✓) gives prefill/decode coefficients 1/6 vs 1/3 derived from the roofline — but it is **design-time architecture co-design on Jetson Orin**, with `crossover`=0 `[self-via-sub]`.
- **Byte-reduction and FLOP-reduction in one system: done, but as an ablation, not a policy.** BitSkip (arXiv 2510.23766, *An Empirical Analysis of Quantization and Early Exit Composition in Transformers*, 2025/10/27, title read back ✓) composes ternary quantization with early exit; I confirmed `memory-bound`, `compute-bound`, `roofline` and `crossover` all occur **0 times** in its saved HTML — it is a composition study, not a runtime selector `[self-via-sub]`.
- Also in the neighbourhood: HAQ (2008.04878, hardware-feedback bitwidth, design-time CNN), DP-LLM (2508.06041, runtime layer precision), QEIL v2 (2602.06057), A-IO (2604.09752), HW-Router (routes *models*, not optimization families).

**What would kill it:**
1. **A serving engine already dispatching optimization *family* on a `num_tokens`/batch/context axis.** vLLM 0.29.0's family-dispatch path is **UNCHECKED** — `api.github.com` is rate-limited to zero here (though `github.com` HTML and `raw.githubusercontent.com` do work). **This is the biggest single hole in the whole sweep and the cheapest to close.**
2. A crossover-conditioned policy over {quantization, pruning/sparsity, offload} with measured speedups.
3. Evidence that the crossover is **not a usable boundary** — e.g. each technique family has its own kernel-level knee, so there is no single crossover *line* in (batch, context) space. I did not find such a paper, but it would be fatal to the premise.
4. The trivialising case: if the answer at n > 28 is simply "turn speculative decoding off", candidate 2 collapses into the already-measured spec-decode decay knob (and into the shipped vLLM/SGLang adaptive-K controllers found under candidate 4).
5. **Most on-the-nose unread artifact:** IEEE Xplore doc **11521682**, *"Strategic Model Compression for Sustainable and Societal AI: When to Prune, Quantize, and Optimize"* — the title alone is the closest thing to candidate 2 that the sweep saw, and **zero words of it were readable** (IEEE blocked). It must be retrieved before candidate 2 is treated as open.

### 2c. Candidate 3 — Prefill/decode partition as a first-class decision → `OCCUPIED`

Occupied twice over, at both granularities, and the stated crux — *derive* the split from the compute/bandwidth asymmetry via a roofline model — is also done.

- **DuetServe** (arXiv 2511.04791, 2025/11/06; ICML 2026 poster 62917) `[self-via-sub]`: *"DuetServe operates in aggregated mode by default and dynamically activates SM-level GPU spatial multiplexing when TBT degradation is predicted."* + *"a partitioning optimizer that selects the optimal SM split to maximize throughput under TBT constraints"* + *"an attention-aware roofline model to forecast iteration latency"*. Checklist: split is a decision variable ✓; live ✓; derived from the live mix (predicted TBT degradation) ✓; asymmetry named ✓; derived **from** the asymmetry via a roofline model ✓.
- **Nexus** (arXiv 2507.06608, 2025/07/09) `[self-via-sub]` reaches the same mechanism from an **independent derivation** (measured cost model + greedy real-time search, not roofline): *"This insight enables us to split a single GPU's resources and dynamically allocate them to prefill and decode on the fly, effectively disaggregating the two phases within the same GPU."* Section headings include "Dynamic SM Partitioning via Cost Model", "Hysteresis-Based SM Repartitioning for Stability", and "Limitation of Static Partitioning". Two independent derivations make the occupancy robust.
- **DOPD** (arXiv 2511.20982, 2025/11/26) `[self-via-sub]` — the cross-GPU version, phrase-level near-identical to the candidate: *"adjusts instance allocations to achieve an optimal prefill-to-decoding (P/D) ratio based on real-time load monitoring"*.
- **The candidate's own foil is confirmed as a deliberate published choice**: Sarathi-Serve (arXiv 2403.02310, title read back ✓) — *"prefills are chunked before being coalesced with ongoing decodes with a fixed token budget."* `[self-via-sub]`
- The negative direction is already answered: *When Does Disaggregation Pay?* (arXiv 2608.03741, 2026/08/04, title read back ✓) `[self-via-sub]`.

**One surviving niche, flagged with a high burden of proof (not claimed).** Medha (arXiv 2409.17264; **cite the abs-page title** *"No Request Left Behind: Tackling Heterogeneity in Long-Context LLM Inference with Medha"* — search renders it as "Medha: Efficiently Serving Multi-Million Context Length…") *"dynamically adjusts chunk sizes as the computational bottleneck shifts from MLP to attention operations"* `[self-via-sub]` — i.e. it adapts the candidate's own foil to a shifting bottleneck. The only remaining niche would be *"the split expressed as a schedulable **token budget** derived from a **closed-form** model of the live mix"*, since DuetServe/Nexus use a cost model plus greedy search and the one closed-form derivation found (arXiv 2601.21351, *Analytical Provisioning for Attention-FFN Disaggregated LLM Serving under Stochastic Workloads*, 2026/01/29, title read back ✓) is for A/F provisioning, not a P/D per-iteration budget. **That exact intersection was not searched.** Recommendation: drop candidate 3 unless re-scoped to that intersection.
