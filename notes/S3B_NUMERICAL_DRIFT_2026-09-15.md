# S3b occupancy check — numerical perturbation of the forward pass and greedy-token flip risk

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Question under check:** When a per-layer *numerical* perturbation is introduced into an LLM's forward pass (not a semantic change to the input, not a weight change — e.g. dropping small attention weights, changing a reduction order, using an approximate kernel), **what determines whether the final greedy token flips?**
**Hypothesis under check:** Flip risk depends on the **structure** of the perturbation (which weights / positions / heads are affected), **not merely its magnitude** (e.g. the total perturbed / dropped probability mass).

**Method note.** Every row in §2 was opened and read by me in this session (or by a delegated sweep whose raw fetched bytes are cached under `.s3b_numdrift/`). Where I could only reach an abstract, I say so. Verbatim quotes are copied characters. Absence claims are phrased "I did not find it".

---

## §1 Verdict

### `PARTIALLY OCCUPIED`

**Artifact that decided it: `Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant: Cross-Precision Output Divergence in LLM Inference`** (OpenReview `QDOKyg7a5e`, verified state: *Decision pending for TMLR*), backed on the structure-vs-magnitude side by **WitCert** (arXiv 2607.28699) and mathematically by **arXiv 2512.07647**.

Why this is the deciding artifact and not a lesser relative: it answers **the question exactly as posed** — numerical perturbation of the forward pass, measured at the **decision** level — and it answers it *against magnitude*:

> "We develop an empirical error-propagation analysis whose central finding is counterintuitive: 22 layers of accumulated body error do not distinguish flipping from non-flipping steps—the outcome depends primarily on the top-two logit margin at the lm_head relative to the directional perturbation between the top-two candidates."

That is a decision-level flip criterion for a numerical perturbation, and it already contains a *directional* (i.e. structural) term, not just a magnitude term. Paired with **MarginGate**'s independent finding that pre-flip K/V error is flat, the negative half of our hypothesis ("not merely magnitude") is public and replicated by two unrelated groups. Paired with **WitCert**'s "Aggressive schemes survive on structure, not per-step fidelity", the *positive* framing of the distinction is also already on the record — though at the task-quality level, not the greedy-token level.

What is **not** on the record (see §4) is a *controlled matched-mass* manipulation of the within-layer structure of a numerical attention perturbation with the **greedy token** as the outcome. That is the surviving slice, and it is narrow.

## §3 What is genuinely taken (so we do not re-propose it)

1. **The decision-level flip criterion for numerical perturbation is published.** "Flip risk is governed by the top-two logit margin together with the directional perturbation between the top-two candidates" — that is §2A#1, plus MarginGate's independently measured margin-conditioned flip rates. Anyone proposing "margin predicts flips" is re-proposing.
2. **"Magnitude does not predict flips" is published, twice, by unrelated groups.** MarginGate: "K/V perturbations remain flat before flips". Precision-invariance paper: "22 layers of accumulated body error do not distinguish flipping from non-flipping steps". Also LLM-42's "boundary-crossing events are rare" framing. The *negative* half of our hypothesis is gone.
3. **A structural term in the approximation-error law is already proved.** arXiv 2512.07647 factorizes attention-output error into τ (a magnitude) × ‖μ_tail − μ_head‖ (a geometry/structure term), and proves TV(P,P̂) *equals* the dropped tail mass for top-k. That kills "TV is unexplained by dropped mass" as a novelty *at the top-k attention-distribution level*.
4. **"Which sparse-attention family wins is structure-dependent at matched budget" is published.** vAttention's complementarity observation (top-k vs random sampling) is exactly a same-budget, different-structure comparison — at attention-output quality.
5. **Structure-over-magnitude is already asserted at the task-quality level.** WitCert: "Aggressive schemes survive on structure, not per-step fidelity"; "no single layer's pollution alone loses anything (0/28)". The *slogan* is taken.
6. **Per-(layer, head, step) TV accounting exists and is Lean-4-checked.** WitCert's meter, plus its P2/P3 follow-ups (typed observability, served-TV law `TV <= tanh(a_q w_thr)`). A generic "we will bound the per-layer attention perturbation and propagate it to the output" pitch is occupied.
7. **"Distributional agreement ≠ decisional agreement" is an observed, named phenomenon.** WitCert measured free-greedy diverging at step 1 while teacher-forced top-1 agreement was 93.8%, and explicitly labels free greedy "unsuitable as a quality metric". The observation that a small TV shift can cascade into a fully divergent generation is also in §2A#1 ("a single token flip cascades into a fully divergent generation") and §2A#4.
8. **Real-system reduction-order → token-flip reports exist in the open** (vLLM #42518 closed, #54035 open) and in practitioner write-ups (M5 Max). "Greedy decoding is not reproducible across kernels/batch shapes" is common knowledge, not a finding.
9. **Depth-localised sensitivity is known.** NeurIPS 2025 "Remarkable Robustness" (early/final layers sensitive, middle robust) and MarginGate B.3 (K-error peaks mid-depth, V-error monotone in depth). "Some layers matter more" is taken.

## §4 What remains, if anything

### Nearest neighbour and what it misses

**Nearest neighbour X = `Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant` (OpenReview QDOKyg7a5e), with MarginGate (2605.30218) and WitCert (2607.28699) as the supporting pair.**

X does A: it establishes, at the decision level and for a *numerical* perturbation, that the flip is decided by the **top-two logit margin** and a **directional perturbation between the top-two candidates** — i.e. it reduces the flip criterion to a *final-layer, two-dimensional* quantity — and it shows that 22 layers of accumulated body error are uninformative. MarginGate independently shows the pre-flip K/V error trace is flat. WitCert shows, at the task-accuracy level, that survival is a **cross-layer cancellation** effect rather than per-step fidelity.

**Under condition C it misses B.** X and its neighbours never *control* the perturbation's within-layer structure at a **fixed total perturbed attention mass**:

- Cross-precision (§2A#1) and batch-shape (§2A#2) perturbations are *unstructured and uncontrolled*: which weights receive which rounding is an artefact of the dtype/tiling, and the experimenter cannot dial "drop this mass from these positions vs those positions".
- WitCert's 28-layer sweep varies **which layers** carry pollution, but at *full* per-layer magnitude, and its outcome is needle accuracy — not the greedy token. Its own note that "no single layer's pollution alone loses anything (0/28)" is a statement about **layer-additivity**, not about within-layer position/head structure.
- arXiv 2512.07647 factorizes the error but is *restricted to top-k truncation of a single attention distribution*. Its key identity `TV(P,P̂) = 1 − e^{−KL(P̂‖P)}` **coincides with the dropped mass precisely because the retained set is the top-k set**. It therefore says nothing about the case that matters to us — a retained set chosen by any rule *other* than "largest weights" — and it explicitly stops at the attention output: "it does not directly measure the effect of truncation on downstream accuracy or task-specific performance." It has no greedy/argmax/decision content at all (0 occurrences in full text).
- vAttention compares top-k vs random at matched *budget*, but reports approximation quality of the attention estimate and end-task benchmark quality — not a per-step flip indicator, and not a per-layer propagation to the final distribution.

**Doing B would differ because** it would replace the magnitude-only diagnostic in current flip predictors (dropped mass, K/V error norm, "22 layers of body error") with a **structural term at the per-layer attention level**: at *equal* total dropped attention mass, the identity of the dropped positions/heads determines the displacement of the top-two logit margin, and therefore the flip. Concretely it would (i) turn "margin predicts the flip" into "margin × perturbation geometry predicts the flip" at intermediate depth, and (ii) yield a **structural** ordering of sparse-attention / KV-quantization schemes at matched dropped mass — a ranking that magnitude-only predictors (dropped mass, per-step TV) should get *wrong*. WitCert's own "KIVI-2bit distorts 21.9% of steps yet retrieves 95.8%" is the indicative symptom that such mis-ranking is real; nobody has measured it on the greedy token.

### First-order magnitude estimate

- **MEASURED (ours, not public):** on Qwen3-4B bf16 / HF eager / 1041-token context, at matched retained-count *n*, oracle top-n retention and random-n retention give total-variation distances to the exact next-token distribution that differ by **3–31×**. `TV / dropped_mass` spans **440× (oracle) to 1323× (random)**. Greedy top-1 **never** flipped under oracle retention even at 23% mass dropped, but **did** flip under random retention at n ≤ 256. A per-layer attention-mass perturbation of only **3.4e-5** already yields **TV = 0.0058** in the final distribution after 36 layers.
- **ESTIMATED:** if flip risk scales with the margin-normalised projection of the perturbation onto the top-two logit-difference direction (the criterion in §2A#1), then at matched dropped mass the flip-probability ratio between random and oracle structure should inherit the structure ratio of that projection. Our measured 3–31× TV separation at matched *n* — and a 440× vs 1323× spread in TV-per-dropped-mass — suggests **at least an order of magnitude** separation in flip risk at matched *mass*, with the strong caveat that this is a TV-to-flip extrapolation, not a measured flip-rate ratio. The clean, defensible claim is only the *direction*: magnitude-only predictors cannot be monotone in flip risk.

### Cheap experiment that would bound it (and its kill condition)

Single model, single context, no training: Qwen3-4B bf16, HF eager, the same 1041-token context, 36 layers.

1. At each layer's attention (or at a designated subset of layers), construct three retention modes at **matched total dropped attention mass** (not matched *n*): (i) **oracle** top-n by weight, (ii) **random** allowed positions with n chosen so dropped mass matches (i), (iii) a **middle-structured** control (e.g. contiguous-window retention with mass renormalised to match).
2. Measure, per mode: final-distribution TV vs the exact/unperturbed run; the greedy top-1 **flip indicator**; and the top-1/top-2 **margin** at the final layer.
3. **Kill condition (structure hypothesis is dead):** if, at matched dropped mass, the flip indicator and TV across oracle / random / window are statistically indistinguishable (flip-rate CIs overlapping at n = 200 prompts, TV ratio < ~1.5×), then flip risk is a function of magnitude alone and the structural term is dispensable.
4. **Confirm condition (slice survives):** TV ratio at matched dropped mass stays > 3× **and** the margin distribution shifts differentially (random mode producing materially more sub-threshold margins than oracle at the same dropped mass). Cost: one model, one forward-pass harness, no decoding beyond the first step per prompt — this is a few GPU-hours, and it re-uses the harness already built for the motivating measurements.

**Secondary, cheaper bound:** re-run the existing oracle-vs-random comparison but *only* record, at matched dropped mass, the **top-1/top-2 margin** at the final layer (no flip counting needed). If margins are identical across structures at matched dropped mass, the whole structural story collapses without ever needing hundreds of flips.
