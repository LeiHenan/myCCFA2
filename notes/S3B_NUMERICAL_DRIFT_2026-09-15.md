# S3b occupancy check — numerical perturbation of the forward pass and greedy-token flip risk

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Question under check:** When a per-layer *numerical* perturbation is introduced into an LLM's forward pass (not a semantic change to the input, not a weight change — e.g. dropping small attention weights, changing a reduction order, using an approximate kernel), **what determines whether the final greedy token flips?**
**Hypothesis under check:** Flip risk depends on the **structure** of the perturbation (which weights / positions / heads are affected), **not merely its magnitude** (e.g. the total perturbed / dropped probability mass).

**Method note.** Every row below was opened and read in this session, or by one of two delegated sweeps whose raw fetched bytes are cached under `.s3b_numdrift/` (subA = determinism/reproducibility, subB = certified-approximation). I **re-verified every arXiv ID by reading it back at `arxiv.org/abs/<id>`** and re-fetched the decisive quotes myself. Where I could only reach an abstract, I say so. Absence claims are phrased "I did not find it".

**Motivating measurements (ours, not public claims).** Qwen3-4B bf16, HF eager attention, 1041-token context: oracle top-n retention vs n random allowed positions gives total-variation distances to the exact next-token distribution differing by **3–31× at the same n**; TV is **non-monotone in dropped mass** (random: dropped mass 0.41→0.62 but TV 0.806→0.193); `TV / dropped_mass` spans **440× (oracle) to 1323× (random)**; a per-layer perturbation of **3.4e-5** in attention mass yields **TV = 0.0058** after 36 layers; greedy top-1 **never** flips under oracle retention (even at 23% mass dropped) but **does** flip under random retention at n ≤ 256.

## §1 Verdict

### `PARTIALLY OCCUPIED`

**Artifacts that decided it: `Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant: Cross-Precision Output Divergence in LLM Inference`** (OpenReview `QDOKyg7a5e`, verified state: *Decision pending for TMLR*) **plus arXiv 2604.13206**, with **arXiv 2512.07647 / 2602.18849 / 2602.13804** closing the structure-vs-magnitude theory.

**Why the question as posed is occupied.** `QDOKyg7a5e` answers exactly it — numerical perturbation, decision level — and answers it *against magnitude*:

> "We develop an empirical error-propagation analysis whose central finding is counterintuitive: 22 layers of accumulated body error do not distinguish flipping from non-flipping steps—the outcome depends primarily on the top-two logit margin at the lm_head relative to the directional perturbation between the top-two candidates."

That is a decision-level flip criterion for a numerical perturbation, and it already carries a *directional* (structural) term. Independently, **MarginGate** (arXiv 2605.30218 — different authors, different group) reports the same negative: pre-flip K/V error is flat; margin is the signal. And **2506.09501** states the criterion in words while declining to model it.

**Why the hypothesis is occupied more than it first looks.** The structure-vs-magnitude distinction is not merely implied — it is **proved three times** on the attention side, each time making the guarantee a function of *which* keys are kept rather than of a perturbation size: the boundary score gap `Δ_k = s_k − s_{k+1}` (2512.07647), the "balanced-mass factor" `θ(p) = 4 max_S p(S)(1−p(S))` (2602.18849), and the KKT support gap `Δ(q)` (2602.13804). WitCert states the slogan outright: **"Aggressive schemes survive on structure, not per-step fidelity."** And `2609.03322` already runs the matched-rate (structure-vs-magnitude) comparison at the **input** level.

**Why it is not fully occupied — and this is now the load-bearing point.** `2604.13206` has *already run the structure-vs-scale experiment at the decision level* and found **against** structure for **dense** directions ("Directional sensitivity is scale-driven rather than spectrum-driven", s_max varying "only by 3×" across five orders of magnitude of singular values). Our structure manipulation is a different object: **sparse and combinatorial over ~1041 positions × 36 layers**. Meanwhile every structure-*sensitive* bound (2512.07647, 2602.18849, 2602.13804) terminates at the attention distribution/output and never reaches the token; the only argmax-aware statement located is a **negative** one in 2605.20868. So the surviving slice is the specific matched-mass, sparse-positional-structure → greedy-flip experiment — narrow, and now carrying a burden: it must explain why it does not contradict `2604.13206`.

## §3 What is genuinely taken (so we do not re-propose it)

1. **The decision-level flip criterion for a numerical perturbation is published.** Margin + the directional perturbation between the top-two candidates decides the flip (`QDOKyg7a5e`); `margin > 2ε ⇒ argmax cannot flip` is standard (`2605.30218`). Proposing "margin predicts flips" is re-proposing.
2. **"Magnitude does not predict flips" is published by at least three unrelated groups.** `2605.30218` ("K/V perturbations remain flat before flips"), `QDOKyg7a5e` ("22 layers of accumulated body error do not distinguish flipping from non-flipping steps"), `2506.09501` ("such fluctuations do not always lead to a token flip"). **The negative half of our hypothesis is gone.**
3. **The structure term is already proved — three times, on the attention side.** `2512.07647` (boundary gap `Δ_k`; exact head–tail identity `τ(μ_tail − μ_head)`; MaxST-optimal partition), `2602.18849` (exact `‖J‖_{∞→1} = θ(p)/τ`, an explicit *shape* functional, contrasted against distribution-independent bounds), `2602.13804` (exponential in the KKT support gap). "Structure, not just magnitude, controls approximation error" is **not** a novel claim at this level.
4. **The slogan is already on the record.** WitCert: *"Aggressive schemes survive on structure, not per-step fidelity."* Plus `2609.03322`'s **rate-matched** HotFlip-vs-random comparison at the input level.
5. **Decision-level structure-vs-scale has already been tested once — with a negative result for dense directions.** `2604.13206` measured *which token wins* over perturbation grids in the (v₁,v₂), (v₁,v₁₀), (v₁,v₄₀₉₆) planes, with Flip Frequency / Region Fragmentation / Zero-Crossing Density, and concluded "Directional sensitivity is scale-driven rather than spectrum-driven", s_max varying "only by 3×" across five orders of magnitude of singular values. **Any claim that direction/structure dominates at the decision level must now explain why it does not contradict this.**
6. **Per-(layer, head, step) TV accounting is done and Lean-4-checked** (WitCert), extended to a typed multi-memory observability contract (P2) and a machine-checked **served-output TV law** `TV ≤ tanh(a_q w_thr)` (P3). A generic "bound the per-layer perturbation and propagate it to the output" pitch is occupied — though all of it terminates at *TV*, not at the token.
7. **"Distributional agreement ≠ decisional agreement" is a named, measured phenomenon.** WitCert measured free-greedy diverging at step 1 while teacher-forced top-1 agreement was 93.8%, and calls free greedy "unsuitable as a quality metric". `QDOKyg7a5e`: "a single token flip cascades into a fully divergent generation". `2605.24168` Thm 1 gives the structural reason (the attention→output map is non-injective).
8. **Per-layer sensitivity is characterised.** NeurIPS 2025 "Remarkable Robustness" (early/final layers sensitive, middle robust); MarginGate B.3 ("K-error peaks at mid-depth and contracts back toward the output; V-error grows monotonically along depth"); `2604.13206` (early-layer "avalanche effect"); `2607.27805` (per-component Jacobian spectral norms). "Some layers matter more" is taken.
9. **Greedy decoding is not reproducible across kernels/batch shapes** is common knowledge, with open and closed bug reports in vLLM (#42518 closed; #54035, #42259, #50136 open), shipped mitigations (batch-invariance docs, `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT`, PR #51292 merged, LLM-42, TBIK), and a practitioner write-up. Not a finding.
10. **The flip-probability law is conspicuously *not* claimed — but the *form* of the law is classical and already settled, which changes how a claim must be framed.** `2506.09501` explicitly declines: a flip "is inherently a stochastic event". MarginGate has **zero** occurrences of Theorem/Proposition/Lemma (verified by grep) and calls top-2 margin "a risk signal rather than an oracle". `QDOKyg7a5e` gives a criterion, not `P(flip)`. To state the law one needs **both** (i) a distribution for the per-step perturbation `ε_pert` induced by the numerical change and (ii) a distribution for the margin at decode steps — and, on this sweep, **nobody supplies either, and nobody joins them.** MarginGate gives a *bound* on K/V drift, not a distribution ("A per-trial linear regression over the pre-divergence window gives a median deepest-layer K-slope within |0.007|/token on all five models, indicating no measurable time-axis drift"); the only margin assets are MarginGate's counting statistic `N(Δ)` and `2506.09501`'s Fig. 3 histogram, the latter FP32-only and measured only *at* divergence points, so unusable as a prior over margins at arbitrary steps. **The join of (i) and (ii) is the empty slot.** Importantly, the *decision-level law form itself is not novel*: Thurstone's discriminal-process/probit model (1927) gives a closed-form argmax-switch probability `Φ(−gap/(σ√2))` parameterised by margin and perturbation scale, and randomized smoothing (Cohen–Rosenfeld–Kolter, arXiv 1902.02918, verified: "We prove a tight robustness guarantee in ℓ2 norm for smoothing with Gaussian noise") gives a certified radius of exactly the `margin vs perturbation` form. The `2ε` statement is the deterministic `σ → 0` degenerate case. **A paper claiming a flip law must therefore instantiate a classical form for LLM serving numerics, not re-derive it** — and the field's silence reflects *measurement difficulty*, not an open invitation.
11. **The `2ε` argmax-stability bound — attribution resolved (correction to an earlier draft of this note).** MarginGate states the bound and cites "(Thinking Machines Lab, 2026; Yuan et al., 2025)", but **neither named source states it**: the TML citation resolves to the `batch_invariant_ops` code README (no bound/margin/ε), and `2506.09501` has zero occurrences of `margin`, `bound`, `Theorem`, `Proposition`; the TML *blog* has zero **prose** occurrences of `margin`, `flip`, `argmax`, `top-1` (the 76 raw-HTML `margin` hits are all CSS — checked individually, so the negative is real and not an extraction artifact). **However, the same inequality IS published**, as **Lemma 6 in arXiv 2406.13187** — which I read directly: `Δ > 2τ₂δ ⇒ argmax preserved`. That paper is a **long-tailed semi-supervised-learning** paper, not an LLM-inference paper, so the bound is **classical and domain-transferred**, and MarginGate's own citation of it is simply wrong. The form is the deterministic `σ → 0` case of Thurstone probit / randomized smoothing (§3.10). **Net: the bound is prior art and must be cited as such; what is not prior art is its instantiation for LLM serving numerics.**
12. **A quantified margin→flip-risk predictor already exists.** arXiv 2608.13756 reports *"flips concentrate at small logit margins, which predict flip risk with ROC-AUC 0.94 on 16,384 positions"*, with a teacher-forced replay that *"ties layers to tokens"* and a per-layer prediction list confirmed 196/196 and 252/252. vLLM PR #55944 reports an empirical cut-off at top1−top2 margin ≈ 0.7. **"Margin predicts flip risk, with a measured AUC" is therefore published** — predicting flips from margin is no longer an open contribution.

## §4 What remains, if anything

### Nearest neighbours and what each misses

- **Nearest neighbour X₁ = `QDOKyg7a5e` (Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant) + `2605.30218` (MarginGate).** They do A: give a decision-level flip criterion for numerical perturbation (margin × directional perturbation) and show magnitude diagnostics are uninformative. **Under condition C they miss B:** in both, the perturbation is *unstructured and uncontrolled* — which weights receive which rounding is an artefact of dtype or batch shape, and the experimenter cannot dial "this mass from these positions" vs "that mass from those positions". Neither ever holds total perturbed attention mass fixed while varying *which* positions are perturbed.
- **Nearest neighbour X₂ = `2512.07647` (+ `2602.18849`, `2602.13804`).** They do A: prove that approximation error depends on *which* keys are kept, via shape functionals (`Δ_k`, `θ(p)`, `Δ(q)`), not on a perturbation size. **Under condition C they miss B:** all three terminate at the attention distribution or attention output; `2512.07647` explicitly concedes it "does not directly measure the effect of truncation on downstream accuracy", and my grep found **zero** decision vocabulary. Moreover `2512.07647` is *restricted to top-k*, where `TV(P,P̂)` **equals** the dropped mass by construction — precisely the case where "TV is explained by magnitude" holds. The case that matters (a retained set chosen by any rule *other* than "largest weights") is outside its scope.
- **Nearest neighbour X₃ = `2604.13206`.** It does A: measures **which token wins** over perturbation grids and tests direction-vs-scale directly. **Under condition C it misses B:** its perturbations are *dense unit directions in 4096-dim embedding space*, varied across the singular spectrum; it never constructs a **sparse combinatorial** perturbation supported on a subset of attention positions at fixed dropped mass. Its negative result is about dense isotropy, not sparse positional structure. This is the load-bearing distinction for us.
- **Nearest neighbour X₄ = `2502.04557` (SPRINTER).** It does A: gives an **exact** law mapping an approximation parameter to next-token-distribution TV — `d_TV(p, p_SPRINTER) = η_FP·d_TV(p,q)`. **Under condition C it misses B:** by construction it is **pure magnitude** (a scalar false-positive rate); structure is absent, and the perturbation is the verifier's accept/reject decision, not attention. It is nonetheless the sharpest existing counterexample *shape*: a final-distribution law that is magnitude-only. Any structure claim must say why attention differs.

### The surviving slice, stated precisely

> X₁/X₂/X₃ do decision-level flip analysis, attention-side structure theory, and decision-level direction-vs-scale sweeps respectively. **Under condition C — a numerical attention perturbation whose *total dropped attention mass is held fixed* while the *sparse positional/head structure of the dropped set* is varied, with the *greedy token* as the outcome — none of them measures the structural term.** Doing B would differ because it would (i) add a structural term to the flip criterion at intermediate depth, converting "margin predicts the flip" into "margin × perturbation geometry predicts the flip"; (ii) supply the decision-level counterpart of the attention-side shape functionals, which currently have no token-level consequence; and (iii) provide a **structural** ranking of sparse-attention / KV-quantization schemes at matched dropped mass — a ranking that magnitude-only predictors (dropped mass, per-step TV, K/V error norm) should get *wrong*. WitCert's own "KIVI-2bit distorts 21.9% of steps yet retrieves 95.8%" is the indicative symptom; nobody has measured it on the greedy token.

**A second, adjacent surviving object — the "law slot".** No retrieved artifact supplies *either* half of a decision-level flip law: a distribution for the per-step perturbation `ε_pert` under a controlled structural change, or a margin distribution at decode steps, let alone their join. The classical forms already exist (Thurstone probit, randomized smoothing; see §3.10), so the open work is **instantiation and measurement**, not derivation. Note this cuts both ways: it means our structural term has a natural home (it would enter as a *shape* parameter of the `ε_pert` distribution rather than as a scalar), and it means a paper that only reports TV ratios without joining them to a margin distribution has not filled the slot either.

### First-order magnitude estimate

- **MEASURED (ours, not public):** at matched retained-count *n*, oracle vs random retention give TV to the exact next-token distribution differing **3–31×**; `TV / dropped_mass` spans **440× (oracle) to 1323× (random)**; greedy top-1 never flips under oracle retention even at 23% mass dropped, but flips under random retention at n ≤ 256; a **3.4e-5** per-layer mass perturbation yields TV = **0.0058** after 36 layers.
- **ESTIMATED (extrapolation, flagged as such):** if flip risk scales with the margin-normalised projection of the perturbation onto the top-two logit-difference direction (the criterion in X₁), then at matched dropped mass the random-to-oracle flip-probability ratio should inherit the structure ratio of that projection. Our 3–31× TV separation at matched *n* suggests **≥ 1 order of magnitude** separation in flip risk at matched *mass*. **Calibration against X₃:** `2604.13206` found only **≈3×** variation in `s_max` across five orders of magnitude of singular values — i.e. *dense* direction buys little at the decision level. So the defensible claim must be stated as a **contrast**: *sparse positional structure should matter much more than dense directional structure; if it does not, our hypothesis reduces to known scale-dependence.* That contrast is sharper and more interesting than "structure beats magnitude".

### Cheap experiment that would bound it (kill condition)

Single model, single context, no training: Qwen3-4B bf16, HF eager, the same 1041-token context, 36 layers.

1. Re-run the existing harness with three retention modes at **matched total dropped attention mass** (not matched *n*): (i) **oracle** top-n by weight; (ii) **random** allowed positions with n chosen so dropped mass matches (i); (iii) a **contiguous-window** control with mass renormalised to match.
2. Record per mode: final-distribution TV vs the unperturbed run; the greedy top-1 **flip indicator**; the final-layer top-1/top-2 **margin**; and — critically — the **projection of the logit displacement onto the top-two logit-difference direction**, since that is what X₁ says carries the decision.
3. **Kill condition (hypothesis is dead):** if at matched dropped mass the flip indicator and TV across oracle / random / window are statistically indistinguishable (flip-rate CIs overlapping at n = 200 prompts, TV ratio < ~1.5×), then flip risk is a function of magnitude alone and the structural term is dispensable. **Stronger kill:** if the TV ratio is ≲ 3× — no larger than the dense-direction ceiling `2604.13206` already measured — then our structure effect is the known scale effect under a different name.
4. **Confirm condition:** TV ratio at matched dropped mass stays > 3× **and** the margin displacement projects differentially onto the top-two direction (random mode producing materially more sub-threshold margins than oracle at the same dropped mass) **and** the effect exceeds the ~3× dense-direction ceiling. Cost: one model, one forward-pass harness, no decoding beyond the first step per prompt.
5. **Secondary, cheaper bound (no flips needed):** re-run the existing comparison recording *only* the final-layer top-1/top-2 margin and the top-two-direction projection at matched dropped mass. If those are identical across structures at matched dropped mass, the structural story collapses without ever needing hundreds of flips.
