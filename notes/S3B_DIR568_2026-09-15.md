## 3. What is genuinely taken

- **The core hypothesis is already a published paper.** RaaS (Findings of ACL 2025) names the pattern — reasoning tokens "emerge, are utilized, and then become unimportant afterward" — and calls them *milestone tokens*, explicitly analogous to lemmas in a proof. "Retains their KV vectors until they are no longer needed" is the commit-and-release lifecycle Direction 5 proposes to test. This is peer-reviewed and pre-dates the current window by over a year.
- **The matched control has been run, and it came back negative for selection signals.** Random Attention (2609.03430) at matched budget + matched prompt protection matches or beats SnapKV, R-KV, VaSE and TriAttention on four models and six reasoning tasks, on **decision-level** accuracy with paired clustered bootstrap + sign test. It also explains *why*: the trace is redundant in text (the model restates what it still needs) and across heads (each head keeps its own copy). This is the strongest possible kill: any commitment-aware policy now has to beat uniform random, not just H2O.
- **Attention concentration on reasoning traces has been measured.** 2605.09490 reports top-20% of tokens capture 56.5% of total attention mass on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B / GSM8K (n=50), "average reasoning chain spans 1,422 tokens". That is the "(b) attention footprint" measurement.
- **Step-indexed attention structure has been measured.** BeaconKV (ICML 2026) identifies Thought Revisiting Tokens at specific decoding steps; LazyEviction (ACL 2026) reports Token Importance Recurrence ("a large proportion of tokens regain high attention after multiple decoding steps"). So "(b) as a function of reasoning step" is taken too.
- **The answer-phase vs think-phase lifecycle distinction is taken.** Crystal-KV (2601.16986) splits the cache into `SlipKV` (maintains reasoning flow) and `CrystalKV` (determines the final answer) under an "answer-first principle", and evicts SlipKV when "its utility expires".
- **The decision-level / soft-metric separation is taken.** 2608.01631 ("Does Accuracy Equal Evidence?") exists solely to separate final-answer accuracy from chain-support/faithfulness under KV compression, across ten eviction methods and one quantization method, with a fixed-trace replay control and a quantization control. It names the phenomenon the "answer-evidence gap".
- **The claim is already shipped in production runtimes.** SGLang PR #23315 (`--strip-thinking-cache`) is **merged**; vLLM PR #39806 (evict thinking blocks from prefix cache) is open with the same mechanism. Both treat thinking tokens as disposable and both go further than Direction 5's framing by *also* discarding the answer tokens cached behind them (RoPE position mismatch).
- **The "commit" framing itself is taken for the agentic case.** CommitKV (2608.07855) does lifecycle-through-commit-transitions with paired before/after-commit deletion measurements.

## 4. What remains, if anything

**Residual slice (thin, and I would not fund it as stated).** The nearest neighbour RaaS does A (identify milestone tokens and release their KV when done) using an *attention-pattern* detector. Under condition C (a single long CoT where a commitment is expressed in *text* rather than in attention — e.g. "so x = 17, therefore …"), it misses B (a *content-level* commitment predicate, e.g. "this step's output has been consumed by a later step and never re-referenced"), and doing B differs because it is checkable without attention instrumentation and so composes with any eviction kernel. But Random Attention's result bounds the payoff: after prompt protection, a signal-free random draw already retains what the model needs, so B's headroom over random is the small residue of "the rare fact stated once and never restated, which reasoning traces seldom produce."

**Magnitude (first-order).**
- *Measured (by others, not by me):* attention on reasoning traces is concentrated — top 20% of tokens hold 56.5% of attention mass, bottom half < 10% (2605.09490, n=50, one 7B model). Random at ~4× compression matches the best scorer (2609.03430).
- *Measured (by others):* the cost of the *inverse* policy — caching thinking tokens that are then never matched — is ~1.3–1.6 GB per dead branch and 65–80 GB at 50 conversations (vLLM #39321/#39806), with a reported cache-hit-rate delta of +6.0 pp and TTFT p50 delta of −1.02 s.
- *Estimated (mine):* the residual headroom for a commitment-aware policy over uniform-random-at-matched-budget is **≤ 4–6 accuracy points** at ~4× compression on a 4B-class reasoning model, derived by taking the observed scorer-vs-random gap *before* prompt protection (Random Attention §5.1: baselines "land within 2.2 points of one another" once the prompt is protected, versus larger gaps before). Labelled **estimated**; the per-token attention mass of the bottom 3–5% of reasoning tokens is **< 1%** (measured, 2605.09490 §3.5) which is why I expect the residual to be small rather than zero.

**Cheapest experiment that would bound it** (fits the stated apparatus: one RTX 4090 24 GB, HF transformers 4.57.6 + torch 2.11.0+cu128, Qwen3-4B bf16, no vLLM, GPU 2/3/5/6 only, no sub-ms latency claims):
Measure **decision-level exact-match accuracy**, not latency. Implement eviction as a position mask over `DynamicCache` in plain HF — no kernel work. On GSM8K (n=200, greedy, fixed budget ≈ 4× compression of the observed trace length) run four arms at *identical* budget: (a) uniform random per head, (b) attention-score/H2O-style, (c) text-commitment predicate (evict a sentence's KV when a later sentence consumes its result and no later sentence re-references it — detect by regex/heuristic on the trace), (d) full cache as ceiling. The bounding statistic is the paired difference (c) − (a) in exact-match accuracy with a problem-clustered bootstrap CI; if the CI contains 0, the slice is dead. This is accuracy-shaped, so the load-300 / 192-core noise on the box does not touch it. **Kill condition: (c) does not beat (a) by a bootstrap-significant margin at matched budget.**

**Note on (c)'s prior:** Random Attention already predicts this result is null, and 2605.09490's finding that "accuracy depends solely on how many tokens are permanently discarded … not on how many remain in HBM" plus the 0–2.5% collapse at 50% budget is a second, independent reason to expect the residual to be small. The one thing neither covers is a commitment predicate evaluated at *sub-trace step granularity while the trace is still being decoded*; both RaaS and Crystal-KV approach it through attention statistics rather than text semantics.

# Direction 6 — Decode dataflow / memory traffic

## 1. Verdict

**PARTIALLY OCCUPIED.**

**Deciding artifact (single-GPU decode attribution):** `https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30571` — *Memory-Bound but Not Bandwidth-Limited: The Physical AI Inference Gap in Batch-1 LLM Decode* (Josef Chen, 28 May 2026). It attacks the exact assumption in the brief — "decode time = weights + KV read, therefore bandwidth-bound" — measures the achieved fraction of peak HBM bandwidth, and shows the account is incomplete with a matched A/B control.

**Co-deciding artifact (per-operator attribution inside decode):** `https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01644` — *A Systematic Characterization of LLM Inference on GPUs*. It gives warp-stall attribution per decode kernel and finds the decode kernels do **not** share one bottleneck: FFN-Up is memory-dependency-bound (58%) while O-Proj/FFN-Down are **execution**-bound (51%, register pressure). That is a measured correction to "decode is uniformly bandwidth-bound".

**Co-deciding artifact (the PCIe TP-vs-PP folk conclusion):** `https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30571`'s neighbour `https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01644` **asserts the folk conclusion as a validated result** ("the memory-bound Decode is better served by Pipeline Parallelism"), while a measured engineering corpus on a real PCIe-only consumer rig (`club-3090`) **overturns part of it**, showing the vLLM custom-all-reduce gate that enforces it is a heuristic, not a hardware limit.

**What is NOT occupied** (and is the surviving slice): I did not find any measurement of decode's byte-level traffic split on a **no-P2P, host-staged, 8-GPU, 4+4 two-NUMA-node** box. The closest measurements are (a) single-GPU only (2605.30571), (b) NVLink + InfiniBand (2507.14392), (c) 4× RTX 3090 with **P2P patched on** (club-3090), or (d) a virtualised Gen1-x4 host (cmp170hx). ## 3. What is genuinely taken

- **"The weight+KV bandwidth breakdown is mis-attributed when you measure it."** Taken. 2605.30571 measures achieved-bandwidth fraction across 44 controlled cells and shows the analytic memory floor is not reached — badly so on fast GPUs (H100 at 27% of its analytic floor vs L4 at 81%) — and isolates a **launch-side term** with a matched CUDA Graphs A/B. The brief's framing ("is the commonly assumed split mis-attributed?") is this paper's thesis.
- **"Decode is uniformly bandwidth-bound."** Taken *and corrected*. 2512.01644's warp-stall attribution splits decode's own GEMMs: FFN-Up memory-dependency-bound at 58%, O-Proj/FFN-Down **execution**-bound at 51% from register pressure. So a per-operator attribution inside decode already exists, and it is not the naive one.
- **"Activation/communication traffic scaling with sequence length."** Taken at model level: 2507.14392 derives and validates closed-form communication-volume models for TP, PP and hybrid as functions of S_p, S_d, hidden size and world size, and profiles message-size distributions. That is the accounting the brief asks whether anyone does.
- **"TP decode is bad over PCIe, use PP instead."** *Not* settled — it is asserted in 2512.01644 ("the memory-bound Decode is better served by Pipeline Parallelism") and simultaneously shown to be partly a **software artifact** on real PCIe rigs: vLLM's `world_size > 2 and not fully_connected` early-return disables custom all-reduce on any PCIe-only rig with >2 GPUs, and forcing it past the gate measured **≈ +15% decode** (independently reproduced at +14.8%) — with a documented prefill cost. The gate's own comment says the NVLink test is a "cheap pre-filter in front of an expensive one". So the conclusion is contested, not settled.
- **"A component consuming bandwidth out of proportion to its utility."** Taken, in the time-not-bytes direction. rtx6kpro: at 16–256 KB "the actual data transfer time is negligible compared to setup, synchronization, and protocol negotiation", with NCCL 6× slower than a one-shot kernel at 64 KB. And 2605.30571: a launch-side overhead, not bytes, dominates on fast GPUs.
- **"A surprising traffic asymmetry on a PCIe box."** Taken, twice, and both are *phase* asymmetries rather than byte asymmetries: the same custom all-reduce is **+15% on decode and −9% TTFT on prefill** (club-3090), and the same PCIe-oneshot kernel is **+7% on 4 GPU same-NUMA but no help on 8 GPU cross-socket** (rtx6kpro).

## 4. What remains, if anything

**Nearest neighbour X does A.** 2605.30571 does A: it measures decode's achieved bandwidth against its analytic memory floor and corrects the attribution, with a matched control, but **entirely on single GPUs** (H100 SXM5, A100-80GB, L40S, L4 — one device at a time, batch-1, 7–8B GQA). It therefore contains **no communication term at all**. 2507.14392 adds the communication term with validated volume models, but on **NVLink H100 + InfiniBand**, where all-reduce is cheap — exactly the regime in which the brief's suspicion ("communication is under-counted") is least visible.

**Under condition C it misses B.** C = a **PCIe-only, no-P2P, host-staged** multi-GPU box with the GPUs split **4+4 across two NUMA nodes**, where all-reduce is bounced through host memory at the measured ~12–13 GB/s for 4 MB. Under C, the per-token communication byte count is small but the *achieved bandwidth at decode-sized messages* is not governed by the same curve as at 4 MB, and the cross-NUMA hop is a different link than the same-NUMA hop. Neither paper measures C; club-3090 and rtx6kpro measure neighbouring conditions (P2P patched on; 4-GPU same-NUMA vs 8-GPU cross-socket) but not the host-staged no-P2P case, and neither reports a byte-level attribution of a decode step.

**Why doing B differs.** It changes the answer to "what should be optimised": if the communication *bytes* are negligible (<0.01% of a decode step's bytes) but the communication *time* is several percent, then the correct lever is message-size/synchronisation shape (fusing the two per-layer all-reduces, or replicating Q-proj to remove a gather), not bandwidth. That is a different intervention than the one the bytes accounting suggests.

**Magnitude — LABELLED.**
- *Measured (by others, on their hardware):* L4 reaches ~81% of its analytic memory floor while H100 reaches only ~27% at ctx=2048 (2605.30571) — a **3× discrepancy in achieved-bandwidth fraction across GPUs on the same workload**. CUDA Graphs buys 1.259× on H100 vs 1.028× on L4 — the launch term is ~23% of H100 decode and ~3% of L4 decode (my arithmetic on their quoted ratios; labelled DERIVED).
- *Measured (by others):* NCCL vs one-shot all-reduce at decode-sized messages: 64 KB → 71.2 µs vs 11.8 µs (6.0×); 16 KB → 14.7 vs 7.7 µs (1.9×) (rtx6kpro). Custom all-reduce gate: +15% / +14.8% decode, −9% TTFT prefill (club-3090).
- *Derived (my arithmetic on their model + the brief's measured number) — **estimated**, not measured by me:* for Qwen3-4B bf16 (36 layers, hidden 2560) at TP>1, 2507.14392's model gives 2 all-reduces per layer per token at h elements = 2 × 36 × 2560 × 2 B ≈ **369 KB of all-reduce traffic per decode token**. Against Qwen3-4B's ~8 GB of bf16 weight traffic per token, that is **≈0.0046% of decode bytes**. But at the brief's measured **12–13 GB/s host-staged**, 369 KB takes ≈**29 µs/token at perfect overlap-free bandwidth**, and the rtx6kpro table shows small-message all-reduce runs at µs-scale *latency* rather than bandwidth, so the realised cost is set by ~72 synchronisation events per token, not by bytes. **The asymmetry to test is therefore: bytes negligible (<0.01%), time plausibly single-digit-to-low-double-digit percent.** I could not find anyone who has measured that ratio on a host-staged no-P2P box.
- *Estimated (mine):* the plausible size of the effect is **2–10% of a decode step** at TP=4 same-NUMA, growing toward the low tens of percent at TP=8 cross-NUMA. **This is an estimate.** The reason to bother measuring is that the two accounting methods disagree by ~3 orders of magnitude on the same quantity.

**Cheapest experiment that would bound it** (bandwidth-shaped, fits the stated apparatus: GPU 2/3/5/6 free, load ~300 on 192 cores, no vLLM, one RTX 4090 usable, HF transformers 4.57.6 + torch 2.11.0+cu128, Qwen3-4B bf16):
Do **not** measure a single all-reduce. Measure an **aggregate loop**, which is a ~1 s quantity and therefore immune to the box's per-op jitter:
1. With `NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1` (host-staged, matching the brief's box), run a fixed-size `all_reduce` over a 2-rank process group for ≥2000 iterations, at message sizes **5 KB, 16 KB, 64 KB, 256 KB, 4 MB**. Report achieved GB/s = bytes × iters / wall time, not per-op latency. Run each on a **same-NUMA pair (2,3)** and a **cross-NUMA pair (2,5)** — that is the 4+4 split, reproduced at TP=2.
2. Compute the per-token all-reduce byte count for Qwen3-4B at TP=2/4/8 from 2507.14392's model (2 AllReduce × h elements × dtype bytes per layer per token) — deterministic from shapes.
3. Divide (2) by the achieved GB/s from (1) to get the projected per-token communication time; compare against a measured single-GPU decode step time for the same model at the same context (a ~10 ms quantity, safely measurable).
**Kill condition: if the projected communication time at TP=4 same-NUMA is <5% of the measured single-GPU decode step, the "communication traffic is under-counted" slice is dead for this model class, and the surviving asymmetry is only the cross-NUMA one (test (1) on pair (2,5) vs (2,3) directly — if the cross-NUMA/same-NUMA achieved-GB/s ratio is within the box's 4–10% spread, the whole slice is dead).**

**Honest assessment.** The *single-GPU* half of Direction 6 is occupied cleanly and I would not pursue it. The *multi-GPU PCIe* half is occupied at the level of "someone has measured all-reduce on a PCIe consumer box" but I did not find the no-P2P host-staged 4+4 NUMA configuration measured, nor a byte-vs-time attribution for it. That is a narrow, hardware-specific slice whose value rests on the fact that the two accounting methods disagree by ~3 orders of magnitude — worth one aggregate-loop experiment, not a programme.

## 3. What is genuinely taken

- **"Scheduling unit = the agent step, not the request."** Taken, and *superseded*: every 2026 system schedules the **program / workflow DAG**, not the step. Explicit first-class-unit claims in Autellix (*"programs as first-class citizens"*), ThunderAgent (*"first-class scheduling unit"*), SAGA (*"the entire agent workflow … as the first-class schedulable unit"*), HexAGenT (*"online-revealed DAG"*), Scepsy, Teola, Parrot, FATE.
- **Dependency-awareness as a distinct problem from independent-request serving.** Taken: Autellix's entire premise is *"existing LLM serving systems ignore dependencies between programs and calls"*; HexAGenT does online-revealed DAG scheduling; 2609.10964 does tail-risk-aware turn *release*.
- **Inter-step prefix/KV reuse as a first-class scheduling resource.** Taken, three independent ways:
  1. **In the objective** — ThunderAgent puts `Cost_recompute` in the cost model and proves `Cost_recompute ∝ c_i²`, deriving shortest-first eviction;
  2. **In the priority score** — ThunderAgent's `S_restore`/`S_pause` are literally `1/c_P` (KV size *is* the scheduling signal);
  3. **In the eviction API** — vLLM #37003 proposes a per-token-range `RetentionDirective` with priority, i.e. the orchestrator explicitly arbitrates cache; SGLang ships session-reference-aware radix cache (#29173, MERGED) and `--strip-thinking-cache` (#23315, MERGED).
- **The measured cost of NOT reusing.** Taken, and measured under matched controls at three different granularities:
  - *time share*: SAGA Fig. 1(a) — 38% of execution time regenerating KV between agent steps, reduced to 8%;
  - *token count*: SGLang #23315 — same probe, flag off = `#cached-token=108`, flag on = `#cached-token=59`; vLLM #37003 comment — 139K–563K redundant recomputed prefill tokens;
  - *bytes*: vLLM #39806 — ~1.3–1.6 GB per dead branch, 65–80 GB across 50 conversations;
  - *bucketed by cause on real traces*: agentic-kv-cache — 33.1% of recompute from gaps < 10 s vs 17.5% from gaps > 5 min.
- **The scheduling objective for dependent requests.** Taken: makespan / task completion time / SLO attainment (SAGA, HexAGenT), P95 flow time with CVaR (2609.10964), program throughput at matched latency (Autellix), JCT (Continuum), plus an explicit fairness metric (SAGA *"Agent Fair Share, a task-completion-time fairness metric with provable bounded-deviation guarantees"*).
- **Prefix-cache-aware routing.** Shipped and, per vLLM #42185's close message, *"landed as a first-class concern in llm-d and AIBrix"*; llm-d's agentic-serving guide names *"zero-recompute state reuse with typed retention"* as the SIG northstar.
- **A negative result on the naive version of the idea.** vLLM #42185 measured cache-affinity *request reordering* at **+0.2%** and closed, with a documented root cause (sync-vs-async base-class confound inflating the original +7.4%). Any new proposal on this axis must explain why it is not this.

## 4. What remains, if anything

**Nearest neighbour X does A.** SAGA / HexAGenT / FATE / AgentSysBench do workflow-level scheduling and cache-reuse arbitration, but every one of them optimizes a **time** quantity: makespan, task completion time, SLO attainment, P95 flow time, latency. Leyline is the only artifact I found that reports a **task-outcome** delta from cache management (*"lifts agentic solve rate by +14.3 pp on debug-gym"*) — and Leyline is a *primitive + policy-space agenda*, not a scheduler that allocates KV/prefix budget so as to maximise solve probability.

**Under condition C it misses B.** C = the reply is not the goal, the task outcome is; and prefix/KV budget is the scarce resource being allocated across a DAG of dependent steps. Under C, none of X schedules on outcome. Concretely, I did not find any artifact whose scheduler priority is a function of *expected task success* (or success-per-GB-of-KV) rather than expected completion time. SAGA's "Agent Fair Share" is still a *time* metric; HexAGenT's risk term is *risk of missing a latency horizon*.

**Why doing B differs.** It changes what the scheduler does when the two objectives disagree: a time-optimal schedule drops an expensive-to-reuse branch that has high success value, an outcome-optimal one keeps it. This is a genuine objective change, not a tuning change.

**Magnitude — LABELLED.**
- **Measured (by others, on their hardware):** the outcome-side headroom of *cache management* is **+14.3 pp solve rate** from a ten-line truncation rule (Leyline, debug-gym). The outcome-side headroom of *task-aware serving* on latency is 29–40% (AgentSysBench). The recompute-side waste is **30 pp of execution time** (SAGA), **139K–563K tokens/run** (vLLM #37003), **~1.3–1.6 GB/turn** (SGLang #23315 / vLLM #39806).
- **Estimated (mine, first-order):** an outcome-conditioned scheduler's headroom over a time-optimal one is plausibly **5–15 pp of task success** at matched KV budget — scaled by analogy from Leyline's +14.3 pp, which was obtained from a *policy* change alone with no scheduling intelligence. **This is an estimate, not a measurement.** I found no artifact that measures it.
- **Kill condition.** If, at matched cache capacity, reordering steps by an outcome proxy changes recomputed prefill tokens by **< 8%** versus FCFS (the gate used elsewhere in this workspace's ledger), the slice is dead — consistent with vLLM #42185's +0.2% and with agentic-kv-cache's finding that the binding constraint is *capacity*, not *ordering policy*.

**Cheapest experiment that would bound it (bandwidth/token-shaped; no vLLM needed).**
Replay a fixed, seeded agent DAG (30–50 steps, tool-interleaved) through HF `transformers` with a hand-written **block-granular prefix cache** (block = 16 tokens) on Qwen3-4B bf16, 1× RTX 4090, at a **fixed block capacity** chosen to force contention. Three arms: (a) reuse ON, (b) reuse OFF, (c) reuse ON with outcome-proxy-prioritised ordering.
Report four quantities, all of which are **countable or bandwidth-side** and therefore survive the load-~300 box's 4–10% spread:
1. **recomputed prefill tokens** (exact counter) per completed task — the direct analogue of the SGLang #23315 `#cached-token` probe;
2. **bytes of KV written** = Σ(blocks × 16 tokens × 2 × n_layers × n_kv_heads × head_dim) — deterministic from shapes;
3. **achieved GB/s** over ≥ 200 repetitions (mean, not tail) for the prefill pass;
4. **steps-to-success / success count** on a small deterministic task set, for the outcome axis.
**Do not** report TTFT/ITL deltas; those are unmeasurable here.
Decision rule: require arm (c) to beat arm (a) by **≥ 8%** on quantity 1 or 2 *and* not lose on quantity 4; otherwise declare the scheduling-objective slice occupied-by-equivalence.

**Honest assessment.** This survivor is thin and I would not fund it as a standalone novelty claim. Its distinguishing axis (outcome vs time objective) is real but not *conditional-differentiating* in the sense this workspace requires: the same hardware/load regime does not create it, and the magnitude rests on an estimate I could not ground in a measurement. The much safer read is that **Direction 8 is occupied end-to-end**, including the measurement the brief asks for.
