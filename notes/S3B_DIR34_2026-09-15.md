# Part A — Direction 3: KV-cache *predictive* management

## A1. Verdict

**`OCCUPIED`** — and occupied on **both** sides of the question.

**Deciding artifact (single strongest):** *On the Limits of Learned Importance Scoring for KV Cache Compression* — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14279>. It is a purpose-built predictor-quality ablation: a 1.7M-parameter multi-horizon, cross-attention scorer (SIP) trained to predict future attention, evaluated against **random** and **position-only** controls at matched retention, and it **loses to both**, with an information-theoretic accounting of why (position carries 0.31 ± 0.05 bits about future attention vs 0.12 ± 0.03 bits for the key content).

**Second deciding artifact (the "does prediction pay at all" bound, measured on hardware):** *When Prediction Doesn't Pay: Recency Is Near-Optimal for LLM Prefix Caches* (<https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.llm-prefix-cache-recency-optimal>, alphaXiv, submitted 27 Aug 2026). It computes the **clairvoyant Belady optimum** and shows LRU reaches 99.4–100 % of it, so the *entire* headroom available to any predictor is ≤ 0.5 points of hit ratio — then runs its own earlier Bayesian predictor and finds it reaches only 75–91 % of opt.

**Why not "partially occupied":** all three sub-questions the direction poses are already answered with measurements —
(i) *does a learned/statistical predictor of future KV utility help?* → yes in low-redundancy retrieval (VarRate, NACL), no in redundant reasoning traces (Random Attention, Limits, Protection);
(ii) *is predictor accuracy the bottleneck?* → no: the computable signal is within ~1 point of the oracle in retrieval (VarRate), position-only beats a full learned scorer (Limits), and at the prefix-cache level recency is 99.4–100 % of Belady (recency paper);
(iii) *does something else dominate?* → yes, and it is identified: **structural protection/prompt survival** (0.11–0.14 F1) dominates **scoring** (0.03–0.04 F1) [2605.18053], while in the offload regime **data transfer** occupies 96.9 %/91.8 % of execution time [InfiniGen, OSDI 2024], and the predictor's own scoring cost is small but nonzero (0.57 % → 2.49 % of decode per eviction round) [Random Attention Table 9].

## A3. What is genuinely taken (Direction 3)

1. **The mechanism itself** — learning/statistically predicting future KV utility to drive cache policy — is taken many times over, including with peer review: ForesightKV (ICML 2026), KVP (ICML 2026), NACL (ACL 2024), LookaheadKV, KVpop, Expected Attention, TokenButler, plus the offload-side InfiniGen (OSDI 2024).
2. **The oracle/upper-bound framing** is taken: Golden Eviction (ForesightKV Table 2), TokenButler's Oracle column, VarRate's gold-span oracle (100.0 vs salience 99.0), the Belady optimum in D3-17.
3. **The predictor-quality ablation** — the crux — is taken: D3-11 (learned scorer loses to position-only and random at matched budget, with 5 seeds and an MI accounting), D3-12 (TOST equivalence of four attention scorers under protection; learned credit adds nothing), D3-9 (random-signal substitution costs 6.5 points in retrieval; oracle headroom ≈1 point).
4. **The "something else dominates" answer** is taken, with numbers, in three separate regimes: structural protection ≈0.11–0.14 F1 vs scoring ≈0.03–0.04 F1 (D3-12); data transfer 96.9 %/91.8 % of execution time in offload (D3-7); predictor compute cost 0.57 %→2.49 % of decode per round on-GPU, and it sets the serving ranking (D3-10 Table 9).
5. **The matched random control is shipped tooling**, not a research contribution (D3-14), and random-vs-smart comparisons at matched budget are already published in *both* directions (D3-8 NACL 2024 says the signal helps; D3-10/11/12 say it does not).
6. **The regime dependence is already documented**, including by the negative paper itself (D3-10's 0.000-vs-0.836 needle result) and by the positive papers' regime (D3-8, D3-9 retrieval).
7. **The "attention sinks / recency dominate" line** is taken at its origin (D3-16, ICLR 2024, with a table showing even *linebreak* tokens work as sinks) and re-confirmed in 2026 (D3-11 position-only best; D3-17 recency = 99.4–100 % of Belady).

## A4. What remains, if anything (Direction 3)

**Honest answer: no new mechanism is available. The residual is a mapping/measurement contribution, and it is thin.**

**Nearest neighbour X**: *Random Attention* (D3-10) shows the selection signal contributes ≈0 in **one** regime (redundant reasoning traces), and explains the mechanism qualitatively — "the reasoning trace protects itself against eviction with redundancy at two levels, in the text (the model restates what it still needs as it works) and across attention heads (each keeps its own copy of the trace)".

**Under condition C it misses B**: it does **not** treat redundancy as an independent variable. Its own Table 3 (needle retrieval: 0.000 vs R-KV's 0.836) shows the effect inverts in the low-redundancy regime, but the paper reports that as a scope limit rather than measuring the crossover. It gets closer than any other artifact: its shared-draw granularity control reports a *qualitative* crossover on the same variable ("Removing cross-head diversity therefore costs nothing on real traces at these budgets… It is load-bearing where the first level is absent (a fact stated once and never restated)") — but it reports two endpoints, not a curve, and only for the granularity axis, not for the selector axis. On the other side, D3-9 measures signal value in the low-redundancy regime at a *single* redundancy level (one random-substitution arm). **I did not find any artifact that sweeps a measurable redundancy statistic and reports signal-value as a function of it**; and **I did not find** one that decomposes a single end-to-end gap into {predictor error, protection/granularity, predictor overhead} inside one harness. (Phrasing: *I did not find it* — not "it does not exist".)

**Doing B differs because** it converts two literatures that currently appear to contradict each other into one commensurable curve, and it yields an observable predictor of *which regime a deployed workload is in* (e.g. self-repetition rate / per-head KV duplication) rather than a binary claim.

**Cheapest experiment that would bound it** (RTX 4090 24 GB, HF transformers 4.57.6 + torch 2.11.0+cu128, Qwen3-4B bf16):
- Harness: `NVIDIA/kvpress` (ships `RandomPress`, `SnapKVPress`, `StreamingLLMPress`, `KnormPress` → the matched controls are free). Qwen3-4B bf16 weights ≈8 GB; 4 K-token KV at 36 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 dim × 2 B ≈ 0.6 GB → comfortable on 24 GB.
- Design: 2 regimes × 3 redundancy levels × 3 policies × 2 budgets. Low-redundancy arm = multi-fact synthetic retrieval (8–16 distinct facts, one mention each); high-redundancy arm = the same facts restated k ∈ {1, 4, 16} times in paraphrases. Policies = {Random (matched), SnapKV, position-only (first-4 + recent)}; budgets = {25 %, 50 %}. Metric = exact-answer accuracy plus the redundancy statistic (n-gram self-repetition rate, per-head KV cosine duplication) so the x-axis is measured, not assumed.
- Run both `enable_thinking=True` and `False` — Qwen3-4B is a hybrid reasoning model, and thinking mode is itself a redundancy generator, which makes it the natural manipulation check.
- Cost: ≈10 s/item estimated on a 4090 (4 K prefill + 256 decoded tokens, one policy/budget/level per item) → 300 items ≈ 50 min; 3 seeds for noise ⇒ **≈2.5 GPU·h estimated**.
- **Kill condition**: if Δ(score − matched random) < 2 points at 25 % budget in the low-redundancy arm across 3 seeds (paired bootstrap CI includes 0), then the signal carries no exploitable information at 4B/4K scale and the whole direction is dead for this hardware. Conversely, if Δ ≥ 5 points appears *only* in the low-redundancy arm with Δ ≤ 2 in the high-redundancy arm, the crossover is real and the curve — not the method — is the contribution. Note the power limit: with 200 items and p≈0.5, a single-arm 95 % CI is roughly ±3.5 points, so a 2-point effect needs ≈600–1000 items to resolve; budget the confirmation run accordingly.

# Part B — Direction 4: inference-time compute allocation

## B1. Verdict

**`OCCUPIED`** — including the crux (matched control), which I expected to be the surviving slice.

**Deciding artifact (single strongest):** *Compute Where it Counts: Self Optimizing Language Models* — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10875> (**ICML 2026**, COMMENTS read back `Accepted at ICML'26`). A learned per-decode-step policy chooses sparsity/pruning/quantization actions, and it is benchmarked **against best-of-500 random schedules at matched net keep-rate** — a strong control, not a strawman. This is precisely the "same controller with the signal replaced by a random signal at equal compute" baseline the direction asks about, and it is peer-reviewed.

**Second deciding artifact (the negative half):** *Thinking Hard, Not Smart: Reasoning Models Fail to Ration Test-Time Compute Across Questions* — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07968>. Adaptive/shared-budget allocation beats a uniform static split for 4/7 models at N=5 and **0/7 models at N=20** ("with an average difference of −5.0 points"), i.e. adaptive allocation *loses* to the best static split once the problem set is large enough.

**Third (peer-reviewed, 2022, and still the cleanest signal-quality datum):** CALM (NeurIPS 2022 **Oral**) reports that a parameter-free adaptive signal "often falls bellow the static baseline, despite enabling per-token exit decisions", while its oracle uses 1.5/1.3/1.2 layers.

## B3. What is genuinely taken (Direction 4)

1. **Learned/adaptive budget controllers across every axis are taken**: per-sample (D4-6 ICLR 2026 bandit; D4-2 oracle-supervised classifier; D4-5 EMNLP 2023 adaptive self-consistency), per-layer/token (D4-3 CALM NeurIPS 2022 Oral; D4-4 MoD; D4-10 DASH; SkipDecode 2307.02628 verified), per-step KV-efficiency (D4-1 ICML 2026), per-question thinking budget on **hybrid reasoning models** (D4-9 DART, EMNLP 2026 Findings), reward-shaped length control (D4-8 CODA).
2. **The matched control — the crux — is taken, multiple times and in strong form**: best-of-500 random schedules at matched keep-rate (D4-1, ICML 2026); Gaussian stochastic router at identical isoFLOP (D4-4); random stopping rule at matched expected samples (D4-5); `RandomSkip` at matched skip rate (D4-10); a random budget with matched expectation plus a fixed-b static arm (D4-2); and a signal substitution that reduces to random (D4-11).

## B4. What remains, if anything (Direction 4)

**Honest answer: the mechanism space is closed. What remains is a methodology-level study, and its novelty is narrowing rather than mechanism.**

**Nearest neighbour X**: D4-1 (*Compute Where it Counts*, ICML 2026) runs the matched-control experiment properly — real learned signal vs **best-of-500 random schedules** at matched budget, plus a tuned static baseline — but on **one axis family** (per-step sparsity/pruning/quantization keep-rate; depth fixed), with no preregistration and no second allocation axis.

**Under condition C it misses B**: no artifact I found combines, in **one preregistered harness**, (i) more than one allocation axis (e.g. samples *and* thinking-budget/depth), (ii) three arms {real signal, random signal, trivial signal} **plus** a best-static arm chosen post-hoc as an upper bound on static, and (iii) controller FLOPs/latency **charged on every arm**. The precedents each cover one corner: D4-1 (i-partial + ii-random + iii), D4-14 (preregistration + matched saving, one signal family), D4-13 (iii, no random arm), D4-7 (best-static grid, no random arm). Phrasing: ***I did not find* that combination** — I am not asserting it does not exist.

**Doing B differs because** every existing "adaptive wins" claim is measured against a *different* control (uniform, fixed-b, a heuristic, or a random draw), so the literature's magnitudes are not commensurable; a single harness with all four arms makes the comparison itself the object of study and forces the controller's own cost into the same units. It is a **measurement contribution, not a new controller** — and it will be judged against D4-1 and D4-14's standards.

# Part C — Cross-cutting notes worth carrying forward

1. **Both directions' crux questions are already answered in print.** D3's predictor-quality ablation exists (D3-11 matched-budget position-only/random vs learned, 5 seeds, with MI; D3-12 TOST equivalence) and D4's matched control exists in strong form (D4-1 best-of-500 random; D4-4 stochastic router at isoFLOP; D4-5 random stopping at matched cost).

The disagreement is **regime**, and the regimes are named by the papers themselves. Any new claim must be scoped by regime or it will be falsified by one of these three.
3. **The strongest published nulls are equivalence tests, not non-significance.** D3-12's TOST at a pre-specified Δ = 0.02 F1, and D4-14's preregistered acceptance gates, set the bar: a new "adaptive doesn't help" result that reports only *p > 0.05* is now below the state of the art.
