# Verification-aware attention / KV access for speculative decoding — occupancy map

**Date:** 2026-09-15 · **Scope:** single-purpose screening. Is there a *genuinely unoccupied, material* research problem in exploiting the fact that the *k* draft tokens verified in one target forward pass form a **correlated group of queries** sharing one KV cache?

**Method note.** Every row in §1 was verified by fetching the artifact myself (arXiv abstract/HTML, raw GitHub source, repo-scoped issue search). Unretrievable items are listed in §5, not asserted. Where I quote a code comment I fetched that file at the stated ref; **I did not accept any quote second-hand**, including from this project's own notes — see §5.6 for two cases where local notes contained *paraphrases presented as verbatim quotes*.

### §1b Engine-source admissions I verified myself (not from any list)

These are the "nobody has raised it" candidates, so they are quoted with `file` + `tag`.

1. **vLLM admits tree-based spec-dec has no support in the attention/forward path.** `vllm/v1/spec_decode/llm_base_proposer.py:1628 @ v0.26.0`:
   > `# FIXME: when using tree-based specdec, adjust number of forward-passes`
   > `# according to the depth of the tree.`
   Grep for `draft_tree|tree_attn|tree mask|tree_mask|tree_spec` across `vllm/` at v0.26.0 returns **no attention-mask implementation** — matches are only `suffix_decoding_max_tree_depth` (a *string-matching* budget, `vllm/config/speculative.py:192`) and unrelated profiler "stats tree" objects. **I did not find any PR implementing a tree attention mask in vLLM.**
2. **One KV slot per (request, position).** `_compute_slot_mapping_kernel`, `vllm/v1/worker/block_table.py:347 @ v0.26.0` maps each token to a KV slot from `positions` + the request's `block_table` row. There is no branch dimension, so **two tree branches cannot both hold KV at the same position.**
3. **Batch heterogeneity is deliberately forced to be homogeneous.** `AttentionMetadataBuilder._init_reorder_batch_threshold`, `vllm/v1/attention/backend.py:651-675 @ v0.26.0` computes a **single class-wide** `reorder_batch_threshold = 1 + (2 if parallel_drafting else 1) * num_speculative_tokens`; `reorder_batch` (`vllm/v1/attention/backends/utils.py:494-528`) then splits the batch by `query_lens > decode_threshold`. The threshold is a **function of config, not of the per-request number of draft tokens**.
4. **Sparse backends *do* account for spec tokens, but only as extra decode rows.** `vllm/v1/attention/backends/mla/indexer.py:295` sets `next_n = self.num_speculative_tokens + 1; self.decode_threshold = next_n`, and `:203` documents a "2D (B, next_n)" per-token context-length layout. This is **chain** multi-token decode, not tree verification.
5. **Hard cap precedent.** `vllm/v1/attention/backends/hpc_attn.py:129`: >3 speculative tokens raises — "HPC attention only supports up to 3 speculative tokens (mtp ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3})".

Each is written in the required form, with the conditional axis, first-order magnitude, and a minutes-long / zero-GPU bound.

### §3.1 The *bound* is missing, not the practice: no one propagates attention-output error to the token distribution at the verification step

> **Nearest neighbour X (SpecAttn, arXiv 2510.27641) does** put the sparse mask on the **verifier itself** — Algorithm 1 line 25: `H^v, O^v <- M_v(S, masks={M_j}_{j=1..m}, cache=K_v, V_v)` under the heading "Verification with Sparse Attention" — and says outright that "speculative execution can be enhanced to provide **approximate verification**". **Nearest neighbour Y (QuantSpec, ICML 2025) does** approximate the verifier's own KV too: "When verifying the drafted tokens using the target model, we utilize both the upper and lower 4-bit representations to reconstruct the KV cache in the higher **INT8** precision" (i.e. verification is *not* full precision — verified by me from the full text). **Nearest neighbour Z (SSV/SpecSA §4.3) does** share one query's sparse layout across a verification group, "trading exact selection semantics for lower scheduling overhead". **Under condition C** = the approximation is applied to the verification-time KV, **they miss B** = any statement of *how far the target distribution moves*. SpecAttn reports only "+15.29% increase in perplexity" and never states a distributional guarantee; QuantSpec has **zero** hits for exactness/distribution-preservation/unbiasedness/rejection; SSV's evidence is benchmark accuracy on a 1B NSA model plus a "precision class" knob, and its "exact" variant is exact only *relative to NSA*, which is not the dense target's function. **Doing B would differ because** the field's guarantee is understood to be a property of the verification step — VeriCache puts it that lossy KV "replaces the model's learned next-token distribution p_full with a different distribution p_lossy... this is a **systematic bias**: every sample is drawn from the wrong distribution, and **no amount of resampling corrects it**", and Windowed-MTP puts the converse: approximating only `q` "leaves the output distribution **exactly p**". Yet the one paper that moved the approximation into verification reports the damage and drops the claim.
>
> **Correction to my own first framing (recorded because it matters).** I initially wrote that verify-side approximation is *unoccupied*. **That is false.** SpecAttn (2510.27641), QuantSpec (2502.10424), "Accelerate Speculative Decoding with Sparse Computation in Verification" (2512.21911) and SSV all put approximation at or across the verification step. What is unoccupied is the **guarantee**, not the practice: the practice exists, it is empirically lossy, and it carries no distributional analysis.

- **Conditional difference axis:** the axis is *conditional on where the approximation lands*, and the literature's own architecture is the evidence — draft-side approximation is provably harmless (rejection sampling corrects it; asserted by MagicDec/SPIRe/SparseSpec-L/VeriCache), verify-side approximation is **practised but unbounded** (SpecAttn/QuantSpec/SSV). No paper I found states this asymmetry as a claim or tests it with a matched dense-KV control.
- **The precise surviving gap — a *bound-transfer* problem.** Three ingredients exist and have **not been connected**:
  1. **An exact drift formula for an approximate verifier.** SPRINTER (2502.04557) Theorem 3.1, verified by me including the appendix derivation: `p_SPRINTER(x) = (1 − η_FP)·p(x) + η_FP·q(x)` and `d_TV(p, p_SPRINTER) = η_FP · d_TV(p,q)`, with "Inevitably, if we resort to approximate verification, we have to give up statistical consistency."
  2. **Runtime-checkable attention-output bounds.** vAttention (ICLR 2026) Definition 4.1: "an algorithm X' is (ε, δ)-verified-X if … Pr(‖X'(x) − X(x)‖₂ / ‖X(x)‖₂ > ε) < δ"; and the top-k TV theory (2512.07647): "TV(P,P̂) = 1 − e^{−KL(P̂‖P)}", "‖Attn(q,K,V) − Attn_k(q,K,V)‖₂ = τ‖μ_tail − μ_head‖₂ with τ = TV(P,P̂)". SpecAttn's own Appendix A.3 even has "(1−p)·‖V‖".
  3. **The cheap dropped-mass statistic**, which already exists in eviction work (contour/dropped-mass allocators, 2608.25230) and in SANTA's `Z_tile` machinery.
  **What is missing is the composition:** no work maps an attention-output bound to a **logit or token-distribution** bound, and therefore none can instantiate SPRINTER's `η_FP` for a *KV-dropping* verifier. vAttention states the obstruction itself: "While directly controlling the resulting errors in per-layer outputs or overall model behavior is mathematically challenging, we can effectively regulate the errors in these fundamental computations." A **relevant impossibility result exists and is unconnected to this problem**: "Sampled Estimators For Softmax Must Be Biased" (NeurIPS 2025) — "all functions that only access a sampled subset of classes must be biased" — which applies to any estimator that sees only a subset of KV entries.
- **First-order magnitude:** the transferable quantity is the **dropped softmax mass** `1 − Z_S/Z_full`, which is (a) computable from partial scores the kernel already materialises, and (b) *linear* in the attention-output bound (both vAttention's and the top-k-TV form). The open question is not the mean but the **tail**: what fraction of verification queries have dropped mass above a tolerance τ. Two measured anchors bound the plausible size: SpecAttn's throughput table shows sparse verification **losing** to dense verification (68.26 → 59.95 tok/s at p=0.97) *and* +15.29% perplexity, while SANTA reaches `S=128` ≈ 0.39% of a 32k KV with accuracy matching SDPA — so retention regimes exist where the output error is small, and regimes where it is not worth doing at all.
- **Minutes-long / zero-GPU bound:** on CPU with `transformers`, take Qwen3-4B (already on the box), construct a chain of `k=4` draft tokens, and for a range of contexts and retentions compute (a) the exact next-token softmax, (b) the next-token softmax under top-n KV retention **at the verification queries**, (c) the total-variation distance between them, and (d) the dropped-mass statistic `1 − Z_S/Z_full` — then check whether (c) is *predictable* from (d). **Predictability is the whole question:** if `d_TV(p, p̃)` is a monotone function of dropped mass across models/contexts, a runtime certificate is constructible; if not, the bound-transfer problem is genuinely open. One forward pass per configuration, no GPU. **I did not run it** (the host was unreachable at the end of the session).

### §3.2 The verification queries' KV sets are *nested by construction* (tree lineage), and vLLM's KV layout cannot express that — while SGLang's cannot either at `page_size > 1`

> **Nearest neighbour X (LongSpec §3.3) does** split verification attention into "cache" (unmasked, FlashAttention) + "speculative" (masked, custom Triton) and merge by log-sum-exp — so the *compute* decomposition along the cache/draft axis is taken. **Nearest neighbour Y (SSV §4.1) does** group verifier queries by *content* (adjacent selected-block overlap 50–90% at 8K). **Under condition C** = the KV cache is *paged* and the draft is a *tree* (EAGLE-3/Medusa-style, `topk > 1`), **they miss B** = a KV **layout** in which the lineage-nesting is representable at all. Verified in source: vLLM `_compute_slot_mapping_kernel` maps one KV slot per `(request, position)`, and `SpecDecodeMetadata` carries no branch field, so sibling branches cannot each hold KV at the same position; SGLang's own arg-group hook restricts `topk > 1` **with `page_size > 1`** to three backends because the two-pass structure is needed. **Doing B would differ because** the nesting is *provable from the draft tree topology* rather than inferred from score similarity: the KV set needed by a node at depth `d` is a **superset** of its ancestor's, so the family of needed sets is totally ordered by inclusion along each root-to-leaf path. A generic top-k selector cannot express a *superset relation* — it only sees per-query affinities — which is exactly SSV's stated limitation ("prefill-oriented kernels may load many KV blocks that are irrelevant to any query" / "decoding-oriented kernels leads to duplicated KV-block loads").

- **Conditional difference axis:** the axis is *paged + tree*, not "better reuse". LongSpec's decomposition is not paged (no `page`/`paged` hit in its full text); SSV is not a tree (batch size 1, NSA, `C≤4` adjacent-query grouping on a flattened order). The pair (paged KV, tree draft) is where the nesting cannot be encoded today.
- **First-order magnitude — the prefix re-read is now *documented*, not hypothesised.** I originally framed the magnitude as confined to `O(k)` draft-token blocks (a `k/L` effect, ~0.8% at 8K/k=64, i.e. negligible) unless the prefix is re-read per query. **Issue #47763 answers this for the sparse path**: "the selected sparse K/V blocks are streamed from HBM **once per draft token** — verify KV-read traffic scales ~`decode_query_len×`" (row 19). So the dominant term *is* multiplied in at least one engine path. **But that path is already claimed** (draft PR #55372, row 20), which kills §3.2 in its original form; the dense-path residue is §3.5.
- **Minutes-long bound (still worth running, and still the cheapest decisive fact):** instrument verification to count **KV bytes read per step**, separating (i) committed-prefix blocks from (ii) draft-token blocks, as a function of `k` and context `L`. If prefix bytes scale with `k`, §3.5 is live and §3.2's mechanism generalises beyond MiniMax-M3; if they are flat in `k`, both are dead regardless of how clean the nesting theorem is. **Blocked in this session** (§1c).

### §3.3 Heterogeneous verification batch composition (different `k` per request, plus plain decodes) has a *config-wide* threshold, and the tree case is explicitly excluded

> **Nearest neighbour X (vLLM PR #5691, closed/unmerged) does** state the problem: "The current implementation does not handle spec/non-spec requests within the same batch." **Under condition C** = requests in one batch carry *different* numbers of draft tokens (which is the normal case once a tree's accepted path length varies, or once an adaptive/dynamic-`k` schedule is used — and vLLM 0.26 *ships* a dynamic spec-dec schedule module, `vllm/v1/spec_decode/dynamic/utils.py`), **they miss B** = a per-request rather than per-config decode/prefill classification. Verified: `_init_reorder_batch_threshold` derives **one** `reorder_batch_threshold` from `num_speculative_tokens` (a config value) and `reorder_batch` splits on it. **Doing B would differ because** heterogeneity is not a tuning problem — it changes *which kernel* a request is dispatched to, so a single threshold necessarily mis-classifies either the short-`k` or the long-`k` requests.
- **Conditional difference axis:** *conditional on* per-request `k` variation. If all requests use the same `k` (today's default), the flaw is invisible — which is why it has survived.
- **Priority-(a) check: has the *stated* blocking reason expired?** PR #5691 was blocked on two named things — cuda-graph support, and "modifying `_num_computed_tokens`, which is a field used by chunked prefill". Both have moved: v0.26's V1 engine ships the multi-mode CUDA-graph scheme and marks `supports_spec_as_decode=True` for several backends. **But the residual limitation is not the one that expired.** vLLM's own design doc now *defines* the property that #5691 could not satisfy: uniform means "all the requests have the same query lengths" (`docs/design/cuda_graphs.md:93`), and spec decode is only "uniform decode" when `max_query_len = 1+num_spec_tokens` for the whole batch (`:18`). So the batch-composition constraint was **elevated into the engine's dispatch invariant**, not removed. This is the good kind of priority-(a) finding: the *reason* changed from "we did not implement it" to "it is now a structural precondition of the fast path".
- **First-order magnitude:** mis-classification moves a request from the multi-token-decode path to the prefill path (or vice versa); a batch that is non-uniform loses full-CUDA-graph replay for the whole batch (`FULL_AND_PIECEWISE` downgrades). The hpc_attn precedent shows the decode path has hard caps (≤3 spec tokens, `hpc_attn.py:129`). Upstream's own numbers: vLLM Issue #49547 (quoted in this project's `pa_out/spec-decode-feature-interactions-sweep.md`, **not independently re-verified by me**) reports 47.5 → 55.2 tok/s (+16%) from a *cudagraph-mode* downgrade triggered purely by the attention backend's support level — i.e. the mechanism is real and worth double digits, but the trigger there was backend choice, not batch heterogeneity.
- **Zero-GPU bound:** pure source — enumerate every backend's `reorder_batch_threshold` at v0.26.0 and check whether any is per-request. (I checked `backend.py:651-675`, `mla/indexer.py:295`, `mla/sparse_swa.py:322`, `hpc_attn.py:143`, `mamba_attn.py:397`, `flash_attn.py:337-344`; all are config-derived constants or class-level `_cudagraph_support`.) A minutes-long GPU confirmation is a batch with mixed draft lengths vs. uniform draft lengths at equal total tokens.
- **Honest caveat:** because `max_query_len` is a *batch maximum*, a batch of chain-drafted requests that all use the same configured `k` stays "uniform" even when individual requests accept different numbers of tokens. So the practical trigger is (i) mixing spec and non-spec requests, or (ii) a **per-request variable draft length** (tree depth, adaptive `k`, dynamic spec-dec schedule — which v0.26 ships at `vllm/v1/spec_decode/dynamic/utils.py`). Both are *configuration-dependent*, which is exactly why this is a conditional axis and not a universal win.

### §3.5 The dense, paged, CUDA-graph-capturable verification path — the residue after §3.2 is killed

> **Nearest neighbour X (vLLM Issue #47763, OPEN, with draft PR #55372) does** state the target almost verbatim — "the selected sparse K/V blocks are streamed from HBM once per draft token — verify KV-read traffic scales ~`decode_query_len×`. A query-parallel (prefill / tree-attention) verify path would load each KV tile into SRAM once and reuse it across the `decode_query_len` query rows" — **and a draft PR already implements the union-of-blocks + per-block row bitmask kernel.** **Under condition C** = the *dense* (non-sparse) verification path, they miss B: #47763/#55372 are scoped to **MiniMax-M3's block-sparse `verify-as-decode`**, and the mechanism they describe is the sparse decode kernel's split-K structure. The **dense** path's structural exclusions are not covered by that PR. **Doing B would differ because** the dense path's exclusions are of a *different kind*: they are not "the sparse kernel is split wrong" but "the engine's verification-group metadata cannot express the group at all".

- **The verified mechanism (v0.26.0, my own reading).** In `vllm/v1/attention/ops/triton_unified_attention.py:935-936`, `BLOCK_M = 16 if num_queries_per_kv <= 16 else next_pow2(num_queries_per_kv)` and **`BLOCK_Q = BLOCK_M // num_queries_per_kv`**; the query grid is `total_num_q_blocks = q.shape[0] // BLOCK_Q + num_seqs` (`:970`). So the `k+1` verification rows of one request are processed in `ceil((k+1)/BLOCK_Q)` separate programs, and (2D path) **each program makes its own full pass over that request's KV**. The KV traffic attributable to the verification group is therefore multiplied by **`ceil((k+1)/BLOCK_Q)`**, not by `k` — a smaller, but real and *query-blocking*-driven factor.
- **The split-K red herring, checked.** The 3D/split-K path (`num_segments = num_par_softmax_segments`, `:1074`) is **disabled whenever `max_seqlen_q > 1`** (`:1047`, part of the `use_3d` conjunction). So split-K is *not* the dominant term for spec-decode verification on this path — which is what makes the `BLOCK_Q` factor visible at all. This is a genuine, if modest, opening.
- **Concrete numbers for the actual box.** Qwen3-4B config (read from `/home/user/models/Qwen3-4B/config.json`): `num_attention_heads=32`, `num_key_value_heads=8`, so `num_queries_per_kv = 4` ⇒ `BLOCK_M=16`, **`BLOCK_Q=4`**. Hence redundancy `ceil((k+1)/4)`: **k=4 → 1.25×; k=8 → 2.25×; k=16 → 4.25×; k=64 → 16.25×.** Note the tension: the factor *grows* with draft length, exactly where the verification group is most worth sharing, and exactly where SSV's own gains appear (`γ ≥ 64`, `C=2`).
- **Conditional difference axis:** *conditional on* `k+1 > BLOCK_Q`. At `k+1 ≤ BLOCK_Q` the group is already one program and there is nothing to recover — which is why the inefficiency is invisible in short-draft configurations and in every test that uses `k=1`.
- **Counter-evidence that caps it (important).** TRT-LLM's own tree implementation states the opposite pressure: "With tree attention, XQA cannot simply take the tokens to be processed in the next round as input. Instead, it needs to take **ALL of their parent nodes** as input. This **incurs additional computation**, but it is unavoidable." A tree's sibling subtrees share almost no KV access set, so group reuse is *weakest* precisely where tree verification is most useful. Any tree-shaped version of B must beat this, and the dense path's `BLOCK_Q` fix does not address it.
- **Minutes-long bound (this is the decisive one):** with Qwen3-4B (already on the box) at a fixed long prompt, run target-only forward passes at `k+1 ∈ {1, 4, 8, 16, 32}` rows and measure KV bytes moved per step with a profiler counter, *dividing out* the `(k+1)` growth. If bytes/(k+1) is flat, `BLOCK_Q` re-reading is not material; if it steps up at each multiple of 4, §3.5 has a measurable target. Zero new modelling, one config sweep, minutes of GPU time. **Blocked in this session** by the box's load average of ~305 and by the unverified vLLM engine run (§1c).

**§3.3 (heterogeneous batch).**
- **Killed if** a per-request threshold is already achievable through the existing `reorder_batch` machinery by simply calling it with per-request `query_lens` — the function *already* sorts by `query_lens > decode_threshold`; only the threshold is global. If a one-line change to a per-request comparison fixes it, this is an upstream PR, not a research problem.
- **Also killed by**: the fact that a closed WIP PR (#5691) addressed the sibling problem and died — the maintainers may simply reject the added complexity as not worth it, and the "batch expansion" overhead may be numerically small at realistic batch sizes.
- **Counter-evidence:** vLLM's `sparse_swa` comment — "sparse_swa has no MQA-vs-dense-MHA routing, so multi-token queries take the prefill path and the decode/prefill split stays at that width" — is a maintainer *accepting* the conflation rather than fixing it.
## §4 What would kill each candidate

**§3.1 (the bound-transfer problem).**
- **Killed by the practice already existing without catastrophe.** SpecAttn (2510.27641) puts the sparse mask on the verifier and QuantSpec (2502.10424) verifies at INT8 rather than full precision; both are published and both are used. If a reviewer accepts "+15.29% perplexity and a throughput loss" as the known price, the honest framing is **"verify-side approximation is a bad trade"**, not "an open problem" — and the bound becomes a nicety. This is the strongest kill and it is empirical, not theoretical.
- **Killed if the transfer is impossible rather than merely missing.** Two results point this way: vAttention bounds per-head attention outputs and says plainly that controlling per-layer or model-level error "is mathematically challenging"; and "Sampled Estimators For Softmax Must Be Biased" (NeurIPS 2025) shows any estimator accessing only a sampled subset must be biased for the full normalizer. If no useful attention-output→logit bound exists, then SPRINTER's `η_FP` cannot be instantiated for KV dropping, and the gap is not a gap but a wall. **I found no paper that resolves either way.**
- **Killed if the dropped-mass statistic does not predict the drift.** The whole runtime-certificate idea rests on `d_TV(p, p̃)` being a usable monotone function of `1 − Z_S/Z_full`. If it is not (e.g. drift depends on *which* mass is dropped, not how much), the cheap checkable quantity is the wrong one. This is exactly what the §3.1 CPU experiment tests, and it is the cheapest kill available.
- **Killed if SPRINTER already generalises.** SPRINTER's `d_TV(p, p_SPRINTER) = η_FP · d_TV(p,q)` is stated for a classifier verifier. If that derivation goes through verbatim for *any* approximate verifier with an error rate `η`, then instantiating it for KV-dropping is a corollary, not a contribution — the remaining work would be only measuring `η_FP` empirically.
- **Weakened by SSV's own result:** SSV reports "negligible accuracy degradation" for its `C=4` shared-index variant on a 1B NSA model — if that generalises, verification sparsity may simply be safe and no bound is needed.
- **Strongest counter-evidence (practical):** SparseSpec (MLSys'26), Vegas (ICML'26), SPIRe, SparseSpec-L, BudgetDraft and VeriCache **all** confine approximation to the draft and keep verification dense/full-KV. The field's revealed preference is to avoid the problem rather than bound it, which is evidence that the bound is hard and/or the trade is bad.
- **Unresolved:** I did not find (a) a distributional-distance measurement (TV/KL/two-sample) between SD-with-approximated-KV and the full-KV target at temperature > 0; (b) an exactness theorem for a sparse/evicted/quantized KV used as the SD **target**; (c) a sparse pass used as a proposal accepted/rejected against a denser pass; (d) an attention-output bound propagated to a logit or token-distribution bound. See §5.2/§5.3.

**§3.2 (paged × tree lineage nesting).**
- **Killed by the magnitude argument above**: if prefix KV bytes read per verification step are flat in `k` (because the prefix is read once per request by a single-pass kernel), the lineage structure has nothing to save and the candidate is dead on arrival. **This is the most likely outcome and must be measured before anything else.**
- **Also killed if** the nesting is already derivable: LongSpec's cache/specs split is *itself* the nesting insight in compute form, and SGLang's `topk > 1` + `page_size > 1` restriction shows the page-level tree path exists for at least three backends — so "nobody can express a tree over paged KV" is **too strong a claim**; the accurate claim is the narrower "vLLM's default flat backends cannot, and no one has measured whether doing so pays".
- **Counter-evidence:** SGLang's own comment that `flashmla / trtllm_mla` "can't express the per-branch tree" — but this text is from this project's notes and **I could not re-verify it verbatim** (§5.6). The parts I *did* verify in `speculative_hook.py` are the `eagle_topk` restrictions, not the cascade rationale.
- **⚠️ KILLED IN ITS ORIGINAL FORM — see §3.5.** The "k verification queries cause k× redundant KV reads" claim is *already an open vLLM issue with a draft PR*. The only surviving part is the narrow dense-path residue in §3.5.

### §5.7 Late correction to my own search coverage

I mapped the **spec-decode axis** first (SSV/SparseSpec/LongSpec/SGLang/vLLM) and only afterwards searched the **exactness axis** directly. That second pass surfaced two artifacts that materially change the picture and that a spec-decode-only sweep would have missed:

### §5.9 Third pass: the exactness axis (this is the pass that changed the answer)

A dedicated recon of the **exactness axis** (rows 26–32, §1a-bis) was run after the first two passes, because the first two only searched the *spec-decode* vocabulary. It invalidated my original §3.1 headline.

**What I had claimed:** verify-side approximation is unoccupied, so a distribution-preservation result there would be new.

**Why that was wrong:** the *practice* is occupied —
- **SpecAttn (2510.27641)** applies the sparse mask to the **verifier** (Algorithm 1 line 25) and says so: "approximate verification". Reports "+15.29% increase in perplexity" and its own matched table shows **68.26 → 59.95 tok/s**, i.e. sparse verification was *slower than dense verification* in the one head-to-head it reports.
- **QuantSpec (2502.10424)** verifies at **INT8** reconstructed from 4-bit halves — "we utilize both the upper and lower 4-bit representations to reconstruct the KV cache in the higher INT8 precision" — with zero exactness discussion.
- **SSV/SpecSA (2605.19893)** §4.3 and **2512.21911** both approximate across the verification step.

**What survives, precisely:** the **bound**, not the practice. Three ingredients exist unconnected — (i) SPRINTER's exact drift formula for an approximate *verifier* (`d_TV(p,p_SPRINTER) = η_FP · d_TV(p,q)`), (ii) runtime-checkable **attention-output** bounds (vAttention's `(ε,δ)`; the top-k TV theory `τ = TV(P,P̂)`), (iii) the cheap dropped-mass statistic. **No work maps (ii) to a logit/token-distribution bound, and therefore none can instantiate (i) with an `η_FP` produced by KV dropping.** An unconnected impossibility result ("Sampled Estimators For Softmax Must Be Biased", NeurIPS 2025) suggests this composition may be hard rather than merely absent — which is why §4 lists "impossible rather than missing" as a kill condition.

**Vocabulary lesson, restated because it cost two passes:** the SD-exactness literature and the sparse-attention literature describe the same object in disjoint terms. The first two passes searched `speculative decoding × sparse attention` and found SSV/SparseSpec/LongSpec; they **missed** SpecAttn, QuantSpec, SPRINTER, VeriCache, vAttention and the top-k TV theory, all of which are only reachable from the *exactness/distribution* vocabulary. Conversely an exactness-only search never surfaces the engine-side story in §1b. **Both vocabularies are mandatory for any occupancy claim here.**

**Also corrected:** the brief's framing that SpecSA is the sole nearest neighbour understates the field. On the exactness axis the nearest neighbours are **SPRINTER** (owns the drift formula) and **SpecAttn** (owns the verify-side practice); on the KV-structure axis they are **LongSpec** (owns the cache/speculative split) and **SSV/SpecSA** (owns cross-query block reuse); on the draft side they are **SparseSpec/Vegas/SPIRe/VeriCache** (own sparse-draft + dense-verify, and the losslessness argument).
