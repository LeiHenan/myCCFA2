### CLOSED-1: HMA is preserved by default for connectors that support it
- URL: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/41847
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Detail: PR #41847 (author chfeng-cs, title "[KV Transfer] Enable HMA by default for connectors that support it") is confirmed merged on the GitHub page and is visible in the local tree as `KVConnectorFactory.supports_hma_config`. The design was worked out in issue #41830, where the author wrote: "My plan is to add a config-level helper that resolves effective HMA support from KVTransferConfig, recursing into MultiConnector children, then use that in the auto-disable path." The shipped code was re-verified on live `main` at https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/vllm/config/vllm.py (lines 2018-2024): `# Auto-disable HMA only when the connector config does not support it.` and `if not KVConnectorFactory.supports_hma_config(self.kv_transfer_config):`. Its warning text states the consequence: "hybrid SSM models (e.g. Jamba, Bamba) require HMA and will fail at startup without it; models with sliding window attention will run with reduced performance."

### CLOSED-2: `kv_load_failure_policy` ships defaulting to `fail`, and is documented
- URL: https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/nixl_connector_usage/
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Detail: The NixlConnector Usage Guide carries a "KV Load Failure Policy" section stating: "`fail` (default): Immediately fail the request with an error when KV load fails. This prevents performance degradation by avoiding recomputation of prefill work on the decode instance." and "`recompute`: Recompute failed blocks locally on the decode instance. This may cause performance `jitter` on decode instances..." The shipped default was re-verified on live `main` at https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/vllm/config/kv_transfer.py (line 103): `kv_load_failure_policy: Literal["recompute", "fail"] = "fail"` with the docstring "'fail': immediately fail the request with an error finish reason (default)". It is also present in the local tree `/tmp/kvsrc/vllm-main`. The dedicated RFC that asked for this change, issue #32200, was nonetheless closed `NOT_PLANNED` by the stale bot — see ABANDONED-3.

### CLOSED-3: KV role flags and the prefiller KV-block lease/heartbeat lifecycle are shipped
- URL: https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/nixl_connector_usage/
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Detail: Documented flags: "`kv_producer`: For prefiller instances that generate KV caches" and "`kv_consumer`: For decoder instances that consume KV caches from prefiller", with "`kv_role="kv_both"` is deprecated for NixlConnector." ### CLOSED-6: Prefill-vs-decode engine-count tuning has shipped, documented guidance
- URL: https://docs.dynamo.nvidia.com/dynamo/v-0-8-1/user-guides/tuning-disaggregated-performance
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Detail: Dynamo's tuning guide has a section "Number of Prefill and Decode Engines" listing three load regimes and states: "At low load, disaggregation would not benefit much as prefill and decode are usually computed separately. It is usually better to use a single monolithic engine." It gives the ratio knob's direction explicitly: "Decrease the total amount of KV cache: Some GPUs are configured as prefill engines whose KV cache is not used in the decode phase. Since Dynamo currently allocates the KV blocks immediately when the decode engine get the requests, it is advisable to use as few prefill engines as possible (even no prefill engine) to maximize the available KV cache in decode engines." It also couples block size to transfer cost: "For block size, if the block size is too small, it leads to small memory chunks in the P->D KV cache transfer and poor performance... For most dense models, we find block size 128 is a good choice." And a live capability gap: "However, chunked prefill is currently not supported in Dynamo (vLLM backend)."

### CLOSED-9: Overlapping per-layer KV transfer with later-layer prefill was quantified and published (HMA-Serve), whose own limitations undercut the pairing
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29986
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Detail: HMA-Serve publishes a "compute-transfer pipeline that overlaps each layer's KV cache transfer with later-layer prefill to reduce time-to-first-token (TTFT)" and "deferred dequantization, shipping raw quantized bytes and reconstructing them lazily on the decode GPU to reduce network bandwidth and HBM usage." Treated as CLOSED because the overlap technique is published with measured results; its hardware is entirely out of reach for this researcher (see HW-2), and its cross-vendor premise is itself a limitation of the technique.

### CLOSED-10: Reducing transferred bytes instead of overlapping transfer was published as a win under bandwidth constraints
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07684
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Detail: SCD states the bottleneck premise plainly: "Disaggregated serving alleviates memory bottlenecks in Large Language Model (LLM) inference but creates a severe communication bottleneck: transmitting high-dimensional Key-Value (KV) caches often dominates time-to-first-token (TTFT)." Its result: "SCD delivers up to 2.65 × TTFT speedup over the oracle consumer prefill and dominates quantization and selective recomputation baselines on the quality--latency Pareto frontier in bandwidth-constrained regimes, while keeping generation quality within 5% F1 of the oracle." Its own limitation is stated in the abstract: reuse "across heterogeneous models (e.g. base and fine-tuned variants) causes semantic misalignment that accumulates over layers, degrading generation quality."

### OPEN-1: KV transfer failures are not reported to the scheduler for Mooncake P2P, so PD requests hang
- WHO is still asking: vLLM issue #55870 (reporter on Ascend A3 with MultiConnector + MooncakeConnector), and an open fix PR #56166 by LH-and-FPGA submitting on 2026-09-09
- URL: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/55870
- Verbatim quote of the asking: "In the inspected implementation, discovery failure and per-request transfer errors appear to lack the scheduler-visible failure handling needed to unblock remote-KV waits."
- What specifically is missing: The scheduler is never notified of decode-side pull failures, so requests sit in `WAITING_FOR_REMOTE_KVS` until client timeout. The open PR #56166 describes the three dropping paths: "`_connect_to_prefiller_bootstrap()` does not propagate bootstrap failure to callers. `handle_new_engine_id()` bare-returns when the remote engine is unavailable, abandoning the pending `PullReqMeta` s. `process_pulling_result()` handles `ok_reqs` but only logs `err_reqs`." It notes the contrast: "NixlConnector already reports both handshake and transfer failures through this mechanism; Mooncake P2P was missing the equivalent wiring."
- Evidence: READ BODY

### OPEN-4: Bidirectional KV transfer's block-alignment guarantee breaks on stripped thinking traces
- WHO is still asking: vLLM NixlConnector Usage Guide "Limitations" section, with a cited open issue #43094
- URL: https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/nixl_connector_usage/
- Verbatim quote of the asking: "Reasoning models with stripped thinking traces — When using reasoning models (e.g. DeepSeek-R1) that produce thinking traces (`<think>...</think>`), D's KV blocks cover the full token sequence including thinking tokens. If the client strips thinking traces from the conversation history before sending the next turn, the prompt P receives will be missing tokens from the middle of what D generated. The block-alignment logic assumes P's prompt is a prefix of D's sequence, so pulling KV blocks from D in this case transfers cache computed for the wrong token positions, producing incorrect results."
- What specifically is missing: "We currently assume the router is able to detect such mismatch across turns." The correctness guarantee is pushed out of the engine into an unspecified external router. A second stated limitation is also open: host-buffer support "is planned for future work."
- Evidence: READ BODY

### OPEN-6: NIXL telemetry throughput conflates overlapped transfers and double-counts across TP ranks, so the KV bandwidth bottleneck cannot be measured correctly
- WHO is still asking: vLLM issue #33170 (reporter) — closed by the stale bot, never answered by a maintainer
- URL: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/33170
- Verbatim quote of the asking: "here vLLM calculates the throughput by the `sum of bytes transferred /sum the xfer time`, but if there are multiple transfers overlapped, we can not simply sum each xfer time as the total duration. In real case, the total duration should be shorter if multiple transfers can be in parallel."
- What specifically is missing: A correct overlap-aware throughput definition, plus per-rank aggregation. A second reporter added: "it treats each rank as independent, so if total bytes_transferred is 1000MB and i have 8 ranks (tp=8), it treats each rank as 125MB and increase count by 8, instead of aggregating 1 transfer of 1000MB". Without this, the CLOSED-2 metric block cannot be used to size the transfer/compute overlap budget.
- Evidence: READ BODY

## HW_RULED_OUT

### HW-1: semi-PD — disaggregated computation with unified storage (server-class multi-GPU, NVLink, cross-node)
- Hardware requirement: Four separate server platforms of 8× datacenter GPUs each, with NVLink inside nodes and 200 Gbps cross-node links. The paper's own motivation also requires ≥2 GPUs to deploy a single 8B model pair.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19867
- Verbatim evidence of the requirement: "The experiments involve four platforms. Server A: one node with eight NVIDIA A100 80GB SXM4 GPUs connected with pairwise NVLink [20]. Server B: four nodes with each equipped by eight NVIDIA A100 40GB SXM4 GPUs. The cross-node bandwidth is 200 Gbps. Server C: one node with eight NVIDIA H200 141GB SXM5 GPUs connected with pairwise NVLink [20]. Server D: one node with eight NVIDIA A800 80GB SXM4 GPUs connected with pairwise NVLink [20]."
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Additional verbatim evidence that the core problem is a multi-GPU problem: "The replicated weights disable the flexible deployment for disaggregated systems. For example, deploying Llama3-8B instances needs at least 2 GPUs."
- Relevance to this subsection: semi-PD's whole thesis is avoiding KV transfer cost by not disaggregating storage — it is the strongest published alternative to KV transfer, and it is unreachable on one GPU.

### HW-2: HMA-Serve — memory-heterogeneous prefill/decode across two different vendors' accelerators
- Hardware requirement: At least two accelerators from different vendors on a RoCE fabric (an HBM-based NVIDIA GPU plus a GDDR-based non-NVIDIA device).
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29986
- Verbatim evidence of the requirement: "We evaluate every system on real silicon: NVIDIA A100-80GB on the HBM side, a four-chip Tenstorrent Blackhole p150 mesh (TT × 4) on the GDDR side, over a 100 Gb RoCE fabric, all serving through vLLM 0.19.1". The cross-vendor premise is stated in the abstract: "Pushed to its most economical form, MemHA serving is inherently cross-vendor, since the best-suited chip for each phase may come from a different vendor."
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Relevance to this subsection: this is the per-layer transfer/compute overlap technique applied to a single-vendor-incompatible hardware pair, requiring a 100 Gb RoCE fabric plus a second vendor's accelerator.

### HW-3: 3DLS — 3D logic-stacked chiplet architecture to physically isolate layer-wise KV transfer traffic
- Hardware requirement: A logic-on-logic 3D-stacked chiplet package with vertical die-to-die interconnects, TP up to 16, and a shared lateral D2D fabric of 512 GB/s. Evaluated by simulator, not on a GPU.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01617
- Verbatim evidence of the requirement: "We propose 3DLS, a logic-on-logic 3D-stacked chiplet architecture that separates traffic classes by routing KV-cache transfers through vertical interconnects while preserving decode-side TP collectives on the lateral D2D fabric." and "We evaluate 3DLS against two planar baselines using a trace-driven in-house simulator that models TP, layer-wise compute and communication, and prefill-to-decode KVT." and "The experiment uses OPT-175B [8], TP=16, and 512 GB/s D2D bandwidth."
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Relevance to this subsection: this is the extreme end of the transfer/compute-overlap axis. Its finding is that the contention between layer-wise KV transfer and decode-side TP collectives is what hurts, and the fix is physical interconnect isolation — no software answer, and no single-GPU answer.

### HW-4: Prefill-as-a-Service — cross-datacenter KV transport over commodity Ethernet between separate clusters
- Hardware requirement: Multiple loosely coupled clusters across datacenters, with standalone compute-dense prefill clusters and local PD clusters, and an internal 1T-parameter hybrid model.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15039
- Verbatim evidence of the requirement: "We present Prefill-as-a-Service (PrfaaS), a cross-datacenter serving architecture that selectively offloads long-context prefill to standalone, compute-dense prefill clusters and transfers the resulting KVCache over commodity Ethernet to local PD clusters for decode." and its evaluation: "In a case study using an internal 1T-parameter hybrid model, a PrfaaS-augmented heterogeneous deployment achieves 54% higher serving throughput and 64% lower P90 TTFT than a homogeneous PD baseline".
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Relevance to this subsection: this is the boundary case that defines how far the KV-transfer-cost question can be pushed (cross-datacenter), and it needs a 1T-parameter model and multiple clusters. Its stated premise also confirms the CLOSED-8 framing: "in practice its deployment boundary is still determined by KVCache transfer."

### HW-5: Mamba1 NIXL P/D accuracy validation — requires an 8×H100 box
- Hardware requirement: 8× NVIDIA H100, plus a 52B model, for the P_TP × D_TP sweep.
- URL: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/45019
- Verbatim evidence of the requirement: "Accuracy parity (GSM8K, 5-shot, exact_match strict-match) across the full P_TP × D_TP sweep on an 8×H100 box" with "Model: ai21labs/AI21-Jamba2-Mini (Mamba1)".
- Evidence: READ BODY
- Relevance to this subsection: validates a shipped feature (CLOSED-5) but on hardware five orders of magnitude away from a single RTX PRO 6000.
