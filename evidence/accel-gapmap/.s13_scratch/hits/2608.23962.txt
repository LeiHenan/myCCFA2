 More GPUs or a Smaller Cache?
 Tensor Parallelism versus KV Compression
 for Memory-Bound LLM Serving

 Srikanta Datta Tumkur, Mehar Simhadri, Anshu Bansal, Jay Iyer,
 Sai Pavan Kumar, Sai Kapil Kumar, Ramesh Nampelly, Raj Dandekar
 
 Affiliation: MIT and Vizuara 

 Abstract

 When an LLM serving deployment runs out of KV-cache room, there are two
well-established ways out, and they come from communities that rarely talk to
each other. The systems community adds GPUs. Tensor parallelism shards the
weights and the KV cache across two, four, or eight devices, buying memory
headroom at the price of an all-reduce on every layer and a hardware bill that
grows with the device count. The algorithms community shrinks the cache in
place, with KV quantisation and eviction keeping a single GPU and spending
a little quality instead. Compression papers report memory ratios, parallel-scaling
papers report throughput curves, and almost nobody puts the two on the same
cost axis, so a practitioner facing a fixed model, quality floor, and latency
target cannot tell which escape is cheaper. This paper builds that comparison.
We place tensor-parallel configurations (degree 1 to 8) and KV-compressed
configurations (16/8/4-bit, keep-ratios down to 0.25) on one cost-normalised
axis, cost per million tokens against latency, using a profiled simulator
calibrated on real A100, A40, and H100 hardware, and we go looking for the
cost-equivalence crossover. We do not find one. Across two models
