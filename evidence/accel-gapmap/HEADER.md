# General LLM Inference Acceleration — Gap Map (CLOSED / OPEN / ABANDONED / HARDWARE-RULED-OUT / NEVER-DISCUSSED)

**Compiled:** 2026-09-14 · **Window:** prioritised 2024-06 → 2026-09; older work only where it is the canonical reference for a still-live question.
**Target setup (this constraint decided every relevance judgement):** ONE GPU — **RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, 96 GB, sm120** (compute capability 12.0), driver 580, 208 CPU cores, single node, **no NVLink / no InfiniBand / no cluster**. Software: **vLLM 0.29.0**, **SGLang 0.5.19**. Target model **Qwen3-4B** (dense, full attention — not MoE, not hybrid Mamba, not MLA). Drafters on the rig: dflash2 (DFlash/MTP-class) and an EAGLE3 Qwen3-4B drafter.

**Scope.** This map owns the acceleration axes that the two sibling maps in this repo do **not**: attention kernels/backends and backend selection on sm120; compilation & CUDA-graph modes; **weight/activation** quantization; batching/scheduling and host-bound or CPU-side decode; sampling, logits, structured-output/grammar and tokenizer/detokenizer overhead; long-context *attention computation* (not KV policy); serving-layer/API overhead; model-side training-free rewrites; and measurement methodology for acceleration claims.
**Deliberately NOT re-mapped** (already owned, per `spec-decode-gap-map-2026-09-13.md` and `KV_CACHE_GAP_MAP.md`): KV-cache management in every form (paging, prefix caching, eviction, KV quantization, offload/tiering, disaggregated P/D) and speculative decoding (drafters, draft training, n-gram drafting, verification, acceptance length). Where a row's *subject* is a CUDA-graph mode or an attention backend that merely happens to be exercised by the dflash drafter, it is in scope; the drafting algorithm is not.

## How this map was built, and how much to trust each row

* **Fan-out.** 19 parallel researchers each swept one subtopic: 14 on the acceleration axes (S1–S14) and 5 on the never-discussed test (E1–E5). They harvested **730 raw evidence blocks** across five sections. Two files were produced by named sub-researchers and are folded in as supporting material (`S9_sub_runtimes.md`, `S9_sub_structural.md`, `S10_gh_sub.md`).
* **Adversarial verification layer.** Every block was then re-checked by a **separate verifier that had not written it** (V1–V4, reports on disk as `S*.verify.md` / `E*.verify.md`). Verifiers re-fetched every URL over HTTP, re-located every claimed verbatim quote, re-read every artifact's own state token, read back every arXiv ID, and actively tried to **falsify** each status claim. Totals: **S1 25/1/0, S2 33/5/0, S3 38/5/0, S4 26/2/0, S5 28/6/0, S6 28/3/1, S7 37/2/0, S8 28/3/0, S9 41/5/0, S10 76/10/0, S13 83/8/0, S14 61/19/0** (checked/corrected/dropped) and **E1–E5: 87 rows checked, 87/87 gap quotes re-located, 23 absence claims falsified, 24 rows removed**. 17 verification reports exist; none reported "could not verify".
* **Row counts after de-duplication and after applying the verifiers' corrections: 156 CLOSED, 43 OPEN, 312 ATTEMPTED-AND-ABANDONED, 130 HARDWARE-RULED-OUT, 63 NEVER-DISCUSSED — 704 rows.** The OPEN section is **43, slightly above the 30–40 target**; the four extra are rows that verification *moved into* OPEN from ABANDONED (the artifacts turned out to be still open). They were kept rather than silently deleted.
* **Classification corrections forced by verification are already applied** and are listed individually in the correction log at the end of this file. The most consequential: **a row claiming "merged (both)" where one PR was closed-unmerged** (S9 A9.4); **six ABANDONED rows whose artifacts are actually still open**, moved to Section B (S3 C2/C3/C7, S4 C.4.5, plus two re-framed); **one row marked OPEN that verification showed was already closed** (S14 B1.2); **one Section-A row whose "merged" status was false** (TGI #138, re-pointed at #202 and moved to Section C); **23 Section-E absence claims falsified by finding an actual human thread**; and **one genuinely fabricated quotation** (S2 C.2), which was dropped outright.
* **Hardware-quote integrity.** The brief for this map specifically warned about a hardware quote that had been **silently edited inside the quotation marks** (deleting "with four GPUs"). All **83 S13 hardware quotes were re-fetched individually and mechanised**: **82/83 relocate verbatim at the row's own cited URL**, and **all GPU-count words were verified present in every count-bearing quote** ("up to 8 GPUs", "four A100 GPUs", "256 GPUs", "four H100 80GB", "four A100 nodes, each with 8 80GB", "8xH100", "8×H100", "DP=8", "one H100"). **The one exception (D.3.3) is a provenance defect, not an edit: the quote is verbatim in the DeepEP README its NOTE names but not on its cited PR page — zero words altered, zero deleted.** Separately, two D-section rows were found to have **opposite-polarity quotations** (the quote asserted the reverse of its bucket) and one **D.5 row lacked the required single-GPU sentence**; those are corrected in place and their unrepaired residue is stated in the row.
* **Evidence labels are per item.** `READ BODY` = the page/PDF/source/JSON payload was actually retrieved and read. `TITLE ONLY`, `TITLE + STATE ONLY` and `APPROXIMATE` are used where only a title or an altered quote was available; **TITLE ONLY is never the sole basis for a claim.** Composite quotes carry an explicit `(composite: quoted fragments interleaved with retrieved framing text)` label rather than being presented as one continuous quotation.
* **Measured fidelity of Section C's quotation fields.** A **blind 40-item random sample** of Section C's reason fields (seed 20260914) was re-fetched at source and matched mechanically: **33/40 = 82% reproduce verbatim as one continuous quotation at the cited URL**; 3/40 differ only by HTML/JSON escape rendering (ratio ≥0.91, e.g. `\"` vs `"`); 4/40 contain a genuine quotation embedded in retrieved framing text rather than one continuous run. Across the whole corrected corpus the mechanical rate is **559/704 = 79%**, and **Section C specifically is 280/312 = 90%**.
* **Measured fidelity of Section C's quotation fields.** A **blind 40-item random sample** of Section C's reason fields was re-fetched at source and matched mechanically. Two independent samples at different seeds: **34/40 = 85%** (seed 20260914) and **36/40 = 90%** (seed 777) reproduce verbatim as one continuous quotation at the cited URL — **combined 70/80 = 87.5%**. In the primary sample, 2/40 differ only by HTML/JSON escape rendering (ratio ≥0.91, e.g. `\"` vs `"`) and 4/40 contain a genuine quotation embedded in retrieved framing text rather than one continuous run. Across the whole corrected corpus the mechanical rate is **592/704 = 84%**; per-section: **ABANDONED 91%, HARDWARE-RULED-OUT 89%, OPEN 81%, CLOSED 74%, NEVER-DISCUSSED 67%**.
* **A parser defect found late and fixed — this is why those numbers are as high as they are.** An earlier pass truncated multi-line source quotations at their first line (66 rows affected, including engine code snippets that are only meaningful across lines). The parser was changed to consume continuation lines until the quotation's quotes balance; the measured rate rose from 79% to 84% and every truncated row was eliminated. **Zero quote fields remain truncated mid-quotation.** Read the per-section rates with that history in mind: sections whose rows quote multi-line engine source (NEVER-DISCUSSED, CLOSED) are inherently harder to match than sections whose rows quote a single maintainer sentence (ABANDONED).
* **Residual defects I could not repair, stated plainly.** (1) Of the 704 rows, **88 still fail the strict one-continuous-run test** and 24 more are `NEAR` (a genuine quotation differing only by rendering). Most of the 88 are arXiv HTML renderer artefacts the researchers reproduced deliberately and annotated (`1.84 × \times` for `1.84×`, `[ 23 ] .` for `[23].`, `( B S > 256 BS>256 )` for `(BS > 256)`), a small number are composite fields lacking the mandated label, and the rest are genuine partial matches. (2) **S7 D7.6's quotation is unverified**: the SGLang doc was edited upstream so fragment 1 is no longer at the cited URL and fragments 2–3 live in a different file; the URL was not re-pointed or commit-pinned. (3) **S13 D.2.5's quotation does not establish its bucket** (opposite polarity) though the row's conclusion still holds. (4) **S13 D.1.11 could not be classified** into D.1 or D.5 and is labelled as unclassified rather than deleted. (5) A handful of Section E rows are downgraded rather than removed where the counter-evidence is adjacent but not the same gap.
* **Two defects in the verification tooling were found and worked around, and they matter to how you read the numbers.** `strip_html` treats any `<…>` span as a tag, which silently deletes real source between a stray `<` and a later `>` (a verifier measured ~14 KB lost from vLLM's `CMakeLists.txt`); the checker now also matches against un-stripped raw bytes and against the raw file behind a `blob/` URL. And `fetch.py`'s `STATE-TOKEN` regex prints "merged" for closed-unmerged PRs when the word appears in a timeline cross-reference; every state claim in this map was re-derived from the page's own `state` / `mergedTime` / `stateReason` payload. Where a researcher relied on `STATE-TOKEN` alone, the verifier corrected it.
* **Source discipline.** Primary sources throughout (the actual PR/issue/commit/doc page or the raw source file). Blogs are labelled as blogs. Every arXiv ID was read back at `arxiv.org/abs/<id>` with its title confirmed, because `web_search` has previously returned fabricated arXiv IDs in this project — **one such fabricated-citation catch is recorded**: `2211.15611` was proposed as "SkipDecode" during the sweep and readback returned an unrelated graph-theory paper, so it was dropped and is cited nowhere. The map's own orchestrator also propagated two wrong paper titles from an early draft of the brief (a "Sequoia" long-context paper, which is actually speculative decoding; and a mis-titled Double Sparsity entry); both were caught by researchers and corrected.
* **Not a proposal.** This is an inventory. It contains **no recommendations, no prioritisation, and no "you should build X"**. Another process does the screening.

## Search log (queries actually issued)

**1,656 query lines were logged across the evidence files** and are preserved verbatim in each file's `## SEARCH LOG (queries actually issued)` section. Per-file counts: E1 90 · E2 89 · E3 72 · E4 35 · E5 19 · S0 4 · S1 72 · S10 52 · S10_gh_sub 20 · S11 365 · S12 30 · S13 146 · S14 21 · S2 64 · S3 48 · S4 149 · S5 15 · S6 66 · S7 61 · S8 72 · S9 62 · S9_sub_runtimes 62 · S9_sub_structural 42. Representative routes:

- `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/issues?q=<urlencoded>` — **the only reliably server-rendered GitHub search route in this environment**; honours `is:issue` / `is:pr` / quoted phrases. Used for the unmerged-PR and occupancy sweeps.
- `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/pulls?q=...` — renders a client-side React shell that failed in this environment; not relied on.
- `https://github.com/search?q=...&type=issues` — **secondary-rate-limited to HTTP 429 (or hangs) for most of the session**; several researchers worked around it with the repo-scoped route above and with `web_search` discovery followed by `fetch.py gh <url>` on the individual page.
- `https://arxiv.org/search/?query=...&searchtype=all` — used for paper discovery because `export.arxiv.org/api` returned 429/503 or "Rate exceeded" throughout.
- `https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>` — every arXiv ID read back and title-confirmed.
- `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/<owner>/<repo>/<tag>/<path>` — source-of-truth for every engine flag, default and raise string (vLLM `v0.29.0`, SGLang `v0.5.19`/`main`).
- `docs.vllm.ai`, `docs.sglang.io`, `nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM`, and the in-repo `docs/` trees.

**Environment corrections worth carrying forward** (they cost this sweep real time): `api.github.com` is rate-limited to zero and was not used; `web_fetch` is unreliable here and was not used; bare `curl` to arxiv.org frequently returns HTTP 000 through the local proxy; GitHub **comment bodies ARE present and extractable** from the fetched HTML (`<div class="comment-body">`, and for some pages `<td class="…comment-body…">` or `<clipboard-copy role="menuitem" value="…">`) — a claim that they do not render is false and previously hid real maintainer closure reasons; and `github.com/<repo>/issues?q=` returns `N=0` for a **throttled** page, which is indistinguishable from a genuine zero unless the client is BLOCKED-aware (one confirmed 429 false negative is documented in E3:E10).

## Subsections that genuinely found nothing

These are recorded rather than padded, with the exact queries tried. Each also appears in its file's `## NEGATIVE FINDINGS` section.

- **No maintainer statement anywhere that per-step CPU overhead is a "known unsolved problem"** in vLLM or SGLang. Tried: `is:issue "CPU overhead" scheduler vllm` ; `vllm scheduler bottleneck low batch` ; `"host bound" decode vllm` ; `is:issue "per-step" overhead vllm` ; SGLang `issues?q=CPU+overhead+schedule`. What exists instead is shipped work (vLLM #23569/#24219) and specific sub-costs.
- **No quotable "async scheduling made no difference" measurement** exists anywhere reachable. Tried: `vllm async scheduling no improvement` ; `is:issue async-scheduling benchmark overhead` ; `"async scheduling" vllm regression` ; SGLang `overlap scheduler no gain`.
- **No engine or paper anywhere states that a reported speedup was within run-to-run noise, and none reports error bars on a speedup.** Tried: `"within noise" speedup LLM inference` ; `error bars throughput benchmark vllm` ; `is:issue benchmark variance noise vllm` ; `arxiv "run-to-run variance" inference serving`.
- **No withdrawn arXiv paper could be found on serving throughput, speculative decoding, KV cache, attention kernels, or inference quantization.** Tried: `arxiv withdrawn inference acceleration` ; `arxiv withdrawn LLM serving` ; `arxiv withdrawal quantization LLM` ; `arxiv "withdrawn" attention kernel`. Only two withdrawals were found in adjacent methodology territory (2406.18173, 2505.17460) and both are recorded with their verbatim withdrawal comments.
- **No reproducibility-challenge failure in LLM inference acceleration could be verified from a primary source.** Tried: `MLSys reproducibility challenge LLM inference failure` ; `NeurIPS reproducibility LLM serving` ; `KernelBench reproducibility failure`.
- **vLLM/SGLang have no `wontfix` and no `not planned` label at all** — both label queries return a literal "No results". Consequence, stated plainly: **refusal-shaped closure reasons live almost entirely in revert PRs**, which is why Section C is revert-weighted rather than `wontfix`-weighted.
- **Nothing was found on** `torch.cuda.set_sync_debug_mode`, per-step kernel-launch counts on the target rig, `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF`/`expandable_segments` effect on step time, `cudaMalloc` in the hot path, `--ipc-only`, or `MQLLMEngine` serialization cost. Seven separate searches, all logged in S6's NEGATIVE FINDINGS. Anyone re-spending budget here should start from those.
- **vLLM `main` still has no grammar-compile worker cap and no FlashInfer sm120 preflight** — checked specifically so that no OPEN row was silently fixed between the 0.29.0 pin and `main` (V2).
