**Critical question per paper:** does it (a) claim/test the SD exactness guarantee — i.e. that rejection sampling with target `p` and draft `q` yields samples distributed exactly as `p` — when KV access is approximated, (b) only report end-to-end accuracy/perplexity, or (c) not mention speculative decoding at all?

**Method.** Every arXiv ID below was confirmed by fetching `https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>` and reading the `dc:title` / `<title>` back off the fetched page. Grep terms applied to each full text: `speculative`, `distribution-preserv`, `exact`, `unbiased`, `lossless`, `guarantee`, `sampling`. Retrieval completeness was checked by confirming `References` / `Appendix` / `Conclusion` markers were present in each stripped text. No ID, title, URL, quote, or number below is reconstructed from memory or from a search snippet.

## NEGATIVE / CONTROL RESULTS: does KV eviction or sparse/quantized KV measurably degrade SD acceptance or output quality, against a matched dense-KV control?

Ranked by evidential strength. I report the control structure honestly, including where the control is weaker than the task's ideal.

### N1 — BudgetDraft (arXiv 2606.00144): "16K collapse" of sparse-draft/full-verify acceptance
Strongest published, quantified acceptance-collapse characterization.
- "naive sparse/full speculative decoding suffers from the sparse/full mismatch as context length grows, causing the acceptance rate to drop quickly."
- "As context length increases, SD with sparse-drafter/full-verifier settings exhibits a rapid acceptance degradation, and at 16K the acceptance becomes near-zero while the end-to-end speedup vs AR falls to ≤ 1×. We term this the 16K collapse."
- Figure 1 caption: "Acceptance collapse of SD (sparse/full) on the GS dataset with γ = 5. Acceptance decreases as context length increases and becomes near-zero in the 8K–16K range across KV settings B ∈ {256,512,1024,full}, where the full setting corresponds to 2048 tokens in this experiment."
- **Control structure / caveat:** the sweep *does* include a `full` arm in the same experiment, which is the closest thing to a matched dense-KV control found. **But** that `full` arm is explicitly "2048 tokens in this experiment", i.e. a budget, not full context — and the collapse is reported to occur "across KV settings", i.e. including `full`. The paper also reports acceptance is **non-monotonic** in budget ("a smaller KV budget can yield higher acceptance than a larger one for naive sparse drafting"). So this result does **not** isolate sparsification as the sole cause of the collapse; it characterizes a context-length-driven failure of the sparse/full configuration.

### N2 — QuantSpec (arXiv 2502.10424) Appendix H + Table 3: sparse-KV acceptance degrades faster than quantized-KV acceptance
A same-model, same-dataset, matched-speculation-length comparison between sparse KV (i.e. **StreamingLLM and SnapKV — eviction methods**) and quantized KV.
- Verbatim: "For fair comparison, Figure 9 illustrates the acceptance rate between the draft and target models as a function of speculation length. We observe that QuantSpec consistently outperforms sparse KV approaches in terms of acceptance rate. Notably, as speculation length increases, the acceptance rate of sparse KV methods degrades much faster, whereas our method maintains high acceptance rates."
- Table 3 (Llama-2-7B-32K-Instruct, PG19, 4k): StreamingLLM 88.87 / SnapKV 93.59 / QuantSpec 92.46 acceptance rate (%), each at its own optimal γ.
- The paper itself flags that the flat Table 3 numbers are misleading: "the acceptance rates shown are for the optimal γ values observed from our hyperparameter search [...] Table 3 effectively compares the acceptance rates of the baselines at very low γ (e.g. 1) with those of QuantSpec at much higher γ (e.g. 6)."
- Also: "for such tasks where the whole context is important, sparse KV cache methods are much more lossy, whereas quantization preserves most of the information in the context."
- **Control structure / caveat:** the "denser" arm is QuantSpec's own **INT8** KV, not FP16/full — so this is not a dense-KV control in the strict sense. It is a matched comparison of two *different approximations* (token-dropping vs. quantization) under identical model, dataset, and speculation-length sweep.

### N3 — SparseSpec-L (arXiv 2607.27735) Figure 2: MagicDec(SnapKV) acceptance collapses at long speculation length
- "both training-based models and training-free static compression methods exhibit a severe speedup crater as k increases, directly caused by the steep decay of α. For instance, on LongBench v2, the speedup of MagicDec early peaks at 1.74× when k = 2. However, pushing the speculation length to k = 16 triggers a catastrophic decline in α down to 6.3%, ultimately degrading the overall speedup to a sub-baseline 0.36×."
- Figure 2 is described as reporting "Performance analysis of Eagle-3 and MagicDec(SnapKV) with variable speculation steps [...] on LongBench v2 and XSum with Llama3.1-8B-Instruct."
- **Control structure / caveat:** the comparison arm is Eagle-3 (a trained draft head), not a dense-KV drafter. So this establishes collapse of a SnapKV-compressed drafter at large k, not a clean sparse-vs-dense delta. I did **not** find a full-KV drafter arm in the retrieved text of this paper.

### N4 — SpecExtend (arXiv 2505.20776): static eviction (StreamingLLM) degrades draft accuracy / accepted length, with an explicit "Full KV Cache" arm
**This is the only paper found that literally includes a "Full KV Cache" control arm in the same table as an eviction method.**
- Claim: "A simple way to improve draft accuracy without retraining is to shrink the draft model's KV cache with an eviction policy such as StreamingLLM [...]. However, with such a static eviction policy, draft accuracy degrades when tasks require finer-grained use of past context, such as the Needle Retrieval task [...], due to loss of important context and increased target-draft divergence."
- Table 1 (Needle Retrieval, Vicuna-160M draft unless noted): Full KV Cache — Perplexity 8.311, Accuracy 0.081; StreamingLLM — 2.435, 0.166; CMR (SpecExtend) — 2.237, 0.823; Retrieval (TriForce, Vicuna-7B) — 2.191, 0.976.
- "while StreamingLLM improves general coherence, it struggles to draft the needle tokens accurately due to loss of global context."
- "This improves the average accepted length by up to 2.55× on inputs of up to 16K tokens, outperforming static eviction strategies."
- Figure 3 (left) is described as showing "acceptance rates for hard and easy tokens, where CMR enables more accurate drafting in both cases compared to StreamingLLM."
- **Control structure / caveat — important and unfavorable to a clean story:** the matched `Full KV Cache` arm is present but has the **lowest** needle accuracy in Table 1 (0.081), below StreamingLLM (0.166) and far below CMR (0.823). The degradation claim is therefore eviction-vs-smarter-eviction (CMR), not eviction-vs-dense. Additionally SpecExtend's own eviction is confined to the **draft** model; its Limitations state "speculative decoding operates on the full KV cache for the target model's forward passes."

### N5 — VeriCache (arXiv 2605.17613): quantitative distributional degradation from lossy KV (target-side)
Measures the damage directly in distributional units, using **Full KV as the control**.
- "VeriCache's KL stays under 0.01 nats (attributable to hardware nondeterminism) while reaching up to 3.82× Full KV's throughput. The lossy baselines (KVzip, KIVI) must trade quality for throughput: on Llama-70B/Pipeline 1 at compression 0.5, KVzip accumulates ~14.4 nats of KL per request—the lossy model emits Full KV's exact output with probability only e^−14.4 ≈ 5×10^−7."
- Sequence-level KL grows roughly linearly in decoding steps under KVzip 4× and TurboQuant k4v3, at temperature 0.5 (Figure 4).
- "On coding and tool-calling benchmarks, functional accuracy (e.g., syntax validity, exact argument matching) degrades sharply even at moderate compression ratios" — with "Function call accuracy [...] drops below 10% under KVzip 4× compaction."
- **Control structure:** Full KV is the explicit baseline, and the metric is KL divergence from the Full-KV distribution plus exact-output probability. **Caveat:** the headline identity claim is greedy-decoding identity, and the throughput/acceptance results are reported for the greedy regime; the sampling-mode guarantee is asserted in one clause ("extends to sampling-based decoding via standard rejection sampling") and is not evaluated.

### N6 — llama.cpp issue #23322: SWA/hybrid KV invalidation coincides with low MTP draft acceptance (NOT a paper)
Retrieved in full from the GitHub issue HTML. Explicitly flagged as non-peer-reviewed, hypothesis-driven, and closed.
- Verbatim: "When running Qwen3.6-27B-MTP (a model with Sliding Window Attention / hybrid recurrent memory) through llama.cpp with MTP speculative decoding enabled, the draft acceptance rate drops significantly (~35-37%) compared to standard attention models, despite MTP being designed for higher throughput. This appears to be caused by KV cache invalidation triggered by SWA/hybrid memory mechanisms, forcing full prompt re-processing and degrading MTP prediction accuracy."
- Reported numbers: "Task 310: draft acceptance rate = 0.36667 (44 accepted / 120 generated)"; "Task 354: draft acceptance rate = 0.83333 (20 accepted / 24 generated) <-- short context, works fine"; "Task 366: draft acceptance rate = 0.35360 (157 accepted / 444 generated)". ### N7 — MagicDec (arXiv 2408.11049): the counter-signal (compressed-KV drafting *raises* acceptance)
Reported for completeness because it runs against the "eviction degrades acceptance" direction. Its own comparison is compressed-KV self-speculation **vs. a small draft model**, not vs. dense KV: "model compression alone is unable to reach 90% acceptance rate, while KV compression achieved significantly higher rates under similar memory constraints"; "Fig. 1 illustrates this phenomenon that if a target LLM speculates itself with a sparsified version of its own KV cache, then it can achieve acceptance rates higher than those of small draft models with a full KV cache." It also reports the opposite for static selection within its own family: "static KV selection methods pre-gather a sparse KV cache for attention approximation during generation. This approach eliminates search overhead but typically results in lower acceptance rates."

## ONE-LINE SYNTHESIS OF THE EVIDENCE

Of the 11 required KV eviction/compression/sparsification papers: **8 never mention speculative decoding at all** (H₂O, StreamingLLM, PyramidKV, Quest, DuoAttention, RazorAttention, SeerAttention, XAttention); **1 mentions it only as future work** (Lookahead Q-Cache); **1 cites it only as related work with no experiment** (MInference 1.0); and **SnapKV is the sole paper that actually runs a speculative-decoding stack (Medusa) under compressed KV — reporting speedup only, with no acceptance rate and no exactness claim.** Every paper that does combine KV approximation with SD confines the approximation to the **draft** and verifies against an unapproximated target KV, which preserves the guarantee by construction — so the guarantee is inherited rather than tested. Only two retrieved works engage the guarantee explicitly under KV restriction: **Windowed-MTP** (states and defends it for a draft-only window) and **VeriCache** (quantifies the distributional damage of lossy KV and restores *greedy* identity by full-KV verification, asserting but not evaluating the sampling case). **QuantSpec is the one retrieved paper that approximates the verifier's own KV (INT8) yet makes no exactness claim at all.** The degradation evidence that does exist is about **acceptance rate and accuracy**, not distributional exactness: BudgetDraft's "16K collapse", QuantSpec's Appendix H (sparse KV acceptance decays faster with speculation length than quantized KV), SparseSpec-L's Figure 2 (MagicDec(SnapKV) α → 6.3% at k=16), and SpecExtend's StreamingLLM-vs-CMR accepted-length gap.
