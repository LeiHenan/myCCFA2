[RFC]: The dynamic-SD K decision is a batch-keyed array index — agreeing on its shape before more signals arrive#54749

Description

seongyun1104
opened on Sep 1, 2026
Issue body actions

Motivation.

On current main (504bb8b0c3) the dynamic-SD K decision is one array index:

# vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py:1321-1324
if self.dynamic_sd_lookup is not None and len(num_scheduled_tokens) > 0:
    num_spec_tokens_to_schedule = self.dynamic_sd_lookup[
        len(num_scheduled_tokens)
    ]

The key is batch size, and that choice is fixed in four places, not one:

  • validate_and_normalize_dynamic_sd_schedule unpacks entries as
    (range_start, range_end, num_speculative_tokens) (dynamic/utils.py:37)
  • build_dynamic_sd_schedule_lookup returns list[int], a dense
    batch_size -> K table (dynamic/utils.py:77-81)
  • the scheduler indexes it by len(num_scheduled_tokens) (sched/scheduler.py:1322)
  • since [Bugfix][Spec Decode] Capture the widest uniform decode batch by default #50488, two capture-planning sites read the same dense table
    (worker/gpu/cudagraph_utils.py:219, config/vllm.py:2067)

The decision site is not short of information — schedule() has the request set
in hand, so per-request num_computed_tokens, acceptance history and queue
depth are all in scope. They are simply not read. Whatever a proposal wants K to
depend on, it currently has to widen that tuple everywhere, or bypass the
schedule entirely.

Six different signals are being proposed for this one decision right now.
Batch size is what ships. Beyond it:

signal where
context length #48944 (mine), widening the tuple to (bs, ctx) -> K
token entropy #54082, gating K between 0 and K at runtime
marginal gain LibraSpec, 2608.08721
expert activation cost EcoSpec, 2607.12696
step-wise efficiency (entropy-controlled length) SparseSpec-L, 2607.27735
learned verification payoff Hybrid Verified Decoding, 2606.01019

Two of those papers converge on the same criterion. SparseSpec-L states it
directly: "extending the speculation horizon can reduce rather than improve
speedup when the marginal acceptance probability falls below the relative
drafting cost."

I am not asking for any of them to be adopted. I am asking whether the K
decision should keep being a batch-keyed array index, given that the next six
proposals all need it not to be.

Update, 2026-09-07 — prior art I should have cited, and a seventh signal.

#38038 (Dynamic SD scheduler decides, draft, opened 2026-03-24) already
answers the question this RFC asks, and I did not find it before posting. It
adds vllm/v1/spec_decode/dynamic/manager.py —
DynamicSpeculativeDecodingManager, which picks K by maximizing
goodput = acceptance_length / ITL over a profiled batch_stats[bs][K] -> ITL
table, refreshes its per-position acceptance rates from live
SpecDecodingStats after a warmup period, and interpolates between measured
batch sizes and K values. It is a policy object, not an array index, and it is
the most developed K controller in the tree.

It is also keyed on batch size alone: step(batch_size) -> int, and the
companion generate_config.py sweeps (batch_size, K) on a fixed dataset to
produce {batch_size: {num_drafts: median_itl_ms}}. ITL is context-dependent,
so in that design context is a constant of the profiling run rather than an
input to the policy.

That is the sharpest form of what this RFC is asking about, and it points the
question at the maintainers more than at me: the one component here that was
built as a policy rather than a table still cannot express a context-dependent
K, and it has been dormant since 2026-05-07. Whether that is the intended
destination for this decision is exactly what I do not know.

Separately, a seventh signal has since been proposed and implemented:
#54801 adds num_speculative_tokens_per_seq_len as a second table
consulted only when the batch-size table is unset, prompted by #54691,
which reports speculation becoming a net loss at 185k context and states that
no per-sequence-length hook exists today. I have commented there rather than
restate it here. That is two new entries to the table above in the six days
since this was opened, which is the part I would ask maintainers to weigh.

Evidence I can offer, with its limits.

I ran the (batch, ctx) case, so the numbers below are about that one signal.
Full provenance, including what I have withdrawn, is in a
ledger;
the per-cell working is in a
companion note.

1. The win localises to a single cell. Position-balanced 2 trials, cache
wiped per launch, cold-start burn, 3 warm-up discarded + 3 measured per cell,
c=256, per-arm stdev at most 1.72 %, Gemma-4-31B-it-QAT-FP8 with its assistant
drafter on one H100 NVL. A batch-only schedule [[1,64,3],[65,128,1],[129,512,0]]
against a (bs, ctx) schedule that differs from it in exactly one cell — at
bs ≥ 129 and ctx ≥ 769, K=3 instead of K=0:

ctx batch-only (bs, ctx) ratio
400 1875.6 1890.7 1.01×
900 1453.5 1874.6 1.29×
1900 1416.6 1848.2 1.30×
4000 1232.8 1680.4 1.36×

The ctx=400 tie is the design working: there both schedules select K=0. The
mechanism is narrow and worth stating plainly — at high batch a batch-only
schedule has to turn speculation off, and context is what tells you when to turn
it back on.

The distinct-K set is {0, 1, 3} for both schedules, so this costs no extra
CUDA graphs; graphs are keyed on K, and the K values are the same ones.

2. A cost model over the same interface gets that cell wrong. I also have a
producer that derives the schedule from a stated cost model rather than typing
it — K* = argmax_K E_accept(K) / (F(ctx) + K·M(bs)), the same marginal-utility
shape LibraSpec and SparseSpec-L arrive at. Run with the coefficients it ships,
sweeping only the acceptance prior across 0.35–0.75 (the campaign observed
44–47 % in the K=3 tier), it selects K=0 at the high-batch tier at every
context. It reproduces the batch-only schedule and would not have found the
cell worth 29–36 %.

That gap closes under recalibration — the per-context coefficient has to be
roughly an order of magnitude larger relative to the per-batch one — not under a
change of functional form. I am reporting it because it is the honest state of
that producer: a stated model, uncalibrated, currently disagreeing with my own
measurement.

3. What I have not established. One model pair, one hardware configuration,
one concurrency, four context points, one differing cell. I have not calibrated
the cost model on hardware. I have not attributed the DSD baseline tax to its
terms — the 2×2 design for that is written and unrun. And the sharpness of the K
optimum at low batch did not clear its own pre-registered bar (#48627, and see
the ledger); the result above is at c=256, which is a different regime.

Proposed Change.

None yet — this asks for a decision, not a design. I have a stake here
(#48944 is the ctx-axis version) and I would rather the shape be agreed before
more signals arrive than argue for mine.

The question is where the K decision should live:

  1. Keep it batch-keyed and let each new signal negotiate its own widening.
    Predictable, and the dense array stays trivially cheap. [Bugfix][Spec Decode] Capture the widest uniform decode batch by default #50488 has just made
    this the assumption in two more places, so this is the status quo by default
    rather than by decision.
  2. Let the schedule declare its own key set, so (bs, ctx) and any later
    key are the same kind of object to the consumers. This is what [Spec Decode] Context-length-aware K in DSD (RFC #48627): extend num_speculative_tokens_per_batch_size with a ctx axis #48944 does
    for one extra key; generalising it is a small change to the validator, the
    lookup and the three read sites.
  3. Separate the decision from the schedule, so that a table, a cost model,
    or a runtime controller are interchangeable producers of K. Two producers
    already exist behind one schedule in my branch, which is the minimum evidence
    for this being more than speculative generality — but two is also the
    minimum, and I would not argue for it on that basis alone.

What I think matters more than which option: nothing in the engine currently
measures the quantity these policies need. Acceptance is exported in aggregate,
but per-(batch bucket, ctx bucket, K) draft and verify latency, first-rejection
position, committed tokens per verification, and K-keyed graph hit/miss are not.
My cost model missed the cell above because its coefficients were guesses; there
is no way to stop them being guesses from inside vLLM today. If only one thing
comes out of this thread, that telemetry is the one I would pick, and it is
independent of which option above wins.

I have opened #54748 for the smallest piece of it, so this is not a request for
someone else to do the work: steps the schedule sends to K=0 build no
SpecDecodingStats at all, so a schedule that has correctly stopped speculating
under load is indistinguishable from speculative decoding not running. That PR
adds a step counter keyed on the selected K and is deliberately independent of
everything proposed here.

Feedback Period.

No deadline. I will not open a PR against any of the three options without
agreement here first.

Any Other Things.

Related: RFC #48627 (the ctx axis specifically, with its evidence section
corrected on 2026-09-01), PR #48944, #54082, #49986, #49548, #48494.

Prior art on the ctx dependence itself: MagicDec
(blog, paper
2408.11049) proposes choosing speculation
amount as a function of sequence length; TurboSpec/SmartSpec
(2406.14066) is the batch-adaptive
predecessor of the schedule API this RFC is about.

Update, 2026-09-13 — this RFC left out the controller that actually shipped, and with it in view the question gets sharper.

My 2026-09-07 update said of #38038 that "whether that is the intended destination for
this decision is exactly what I do not know." It is answerable, and I should have
answered it here rather than leaving it open: the destination was #47808
([Spec Decode] DSpark confidence-scheduled verification, merged 2026-08-12), which
added vllm/v1/worker/gpu/spec_decode/adaptive_verification.py. RFC #48627 cites it;
this RFC does not, which makes the Motivation section above read as though the dense
batch_size -> K array were the only controller in the tree. It is not, and the
omission is mine.

#38038 and #47808 are the same author's two answers to this RFC's question. Both are
@LucasWilkinson's. Both choose a speculation budget by maximizing goodput. Both price
context once, before serving, and then hold it fixed.

#38038 (open, draft, dormant since 2026-05-07) #47808 (merged 2026-08-12)
objective acceptance_length / ITL estimated accepted tokens / cost
cost table keyed on batch_stats[bs][K] -> ITL num_reqs, num_target_tokens
context constant of the profiling run constant of the profiling run

For #47808 that is visible in three places. The budget is a goodput argmax
(adaptive_verification.py:342):

draft_budget = int(np.argmax(num_tokens_to_estimated_accepted_tokens / costs))

costs is assembled from two profiled curves, neither of which carries a context axis —
set_initial_cost_curves builds draft_curve keyed on s.num_reqs and verify_curve
keyed on s.num_target_tokens:

        costs = (
            draft_cost_ms[len(req_ids)]
            + verify_cost_ms[
                num_non_draft_tokens_total : num_non_draft_tokens_total
                + max_draft_budget
                + 1
            ]
        )

And context enters exactly once, in batches_to_profile, held fixed across every sample:

                yield {
                    "num_tokens": num_tokens,
                    "context_len": envs.VLLM_ADAPTIVE_VERIFICATION_PROFILE_CONTEXT_LEN,
                }

That env var defaults to 8192. Its comment in envs.py states the consequence directly:
"Raise it for long-context deployments, where step cost is dominated by attention over
a much larger KV cache than the default assumes." The feature documentation says the
same thing to operators: "Deployments serving much longer contexts may want to raise it
so the profiled step reads a more realistic amount of cache."

So context is not absent from the shipped controller. It is a deployment-wide scalar
set before serving, not an input the policy reads per step — which is the distinction
this RFC exists to ask about, and I had been asking it against the weaker example.

To be precise about scope, since it would be easy to overstate: adaptive verification
does not choose K. Drafting still happens at the scheduled length; it chooses how much
of the already-drafted block to verify, scoring every (request, position) slot by
survival probability and admitting the best until the budget is spent. So the array
index above is still the K decision. But "should a table, a cost model, or a runtime
controller produce the budget" is not an open question in the way I framed it — a cost
model shipped in August, and 41 open PRs reference it today.

The question I would actually ask, now: the person who owns this decision has
implemented it twice, three months apart, in two subsystems, and both times context was
priced once at startup rather than read as an input. @LucasWilkinson — is that a
deliberate boundary?

It is a real question and either answer is useful to me. If it is deliberate, then
#48944 and #54801 are both arguing against a decision that has already been made twice
by the person best placed to make it, and the honest thing is for me to say so on my own
PR. If it is not deliberate, then the natural place for a context term is
#47808's cost curves rather than the config tuple I have been widening — a smaller and
better-located change than the one I have been proposing.

I have no measurement on the adaptive-verification path and am not claiming one.

Two smaller corrections while I am here.

Coordinates in the Motivation section have drifted. Refreshed against main
(fa1b3b1922): the scheduler index is now sched/scheduler.py:1393-1397 (was
:1321-1324); the capture-planning reads are worker/gpu/cudagraph_utils.py:246 (was
:219) and config/vllm.py:2282 (was :2067). dynamic/utils.py:37 and :77-81 are
unchanged, and the quoted block itself is unchanged.

One piece of external evidence has arrived. In #54691, @hongboshi1234 measured a
break-even acceptance surface on B300 / TP8 / DCP-8 with Kimi-K3 and a DFlash drafter —
different vendor, model family and drafter from mine — by forcing every draft to be
rejected and comparing per-token latency against no speculation. For any achievable
accept length in (1.91, 3.51), the resulting pays / does-not-pay region cannot be
produced by a context rule and a batch rule consulted separately: (2k, B=8) pays, so
neither rule fires there; (32k, B=8) does not pay, so the context rule must fire at 32k;
but then (32k, B=1) would be switched off, and it pays. My own result has the opposite
sign — speculation switched back on at high batch when context is long — and the same
non-separable shape. Two measurements is not many, but they are independent, and the
second was not looking for this. I checked their proposed mechanism and it does not
hold, which I have said in that thread; the surface stands, the explanation for its
magnitude is open.
